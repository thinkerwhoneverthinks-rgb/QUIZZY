Cell)

EMF of Cell
= E°(reduction, +ve electrode) − E°(reduction, −ve electrode)
Daniell cell:
= (+0.34) − (−0.76) = +1.10 V
E°cell > 0 → spontaneous cell reaction
EMF = electromotive force (potential difference when no current flows).

4
Nernst Equation
E = E° – (RT/nF) ln Q
At 298 K:
E = E° – (0.0592/n) log Q
For a cell: aA + bB → cC + dD
Q = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ
At equilibrium: E = 0, Q = K
∴ E° = (0.0592/n) log K
R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1
F = 96485 C mol-1 (Faraday's constant)
n = number of electrons transferred in balanced cell reaction
T = temperature in Kelvin
Increasing concentration of cathode ion → E increases.
5
Gibbs Energy & Cell
EMF
ΔG = –nFE
ΔG° = –nFE°
ΔG° = –RT ln K
∴ nFE° = RT ln K
E° = (RT/nF) ln K = (0.0592/n) log K (at 298 K)
E° > 0 ↔ ΔG° < 0 ↔ K > 1 (spontaneous)
n = moles of electrons; F = 96485 C mol-1.
ΔG < 0 → spontaneous → galvanic cell.
ΔG > 0 → non-spontaneous → electrolytic cell needed.
ΔG = 0 → equilibrium (cell stops producing current).
6
Conductance &
Resistivity
Resistance: R = ρ (l/A)
Conductance: G = 1/R (unit: siemens, S)
Resistivity: ρ = R·A/l (unit: Ω m)
Conductivity: k = 1/ρ = G·(l/A) (unit: S m-1)
Cell constant: G* = l/A (unit: m-1)
∴ k = G × G*
l = distance between electrodes; A = cross- sectional area.
Cell constant (G*) is determined using a known conductivity solution (KCl).
Conductivity of electrolytic solutions > pure water.
7
Molar Conductivity (Λm)
Λm = k / C
Where C = concentration in mol m-3
If C in mol L-1:
Λm = (κ × 1000) / C (S cm2 mol-1)
Unit: S m2 mol-1 or S cm2 mol-1
Λm increases as dilution increases
Molar conductivity = conductance of all ions from 1 mol electrolyte.
For strong electrolytes: Λm rises sharply with dilution.
For weak electrolytes: Λm rises slowly at
first, then sharply near infinite dilution.
8
Kohlrausch's Law of Independent
Migration of Ions
Λ°m = ν+λ°+ + ν-λ°-
λ°+, λ°- = limiting molar conductivities of cation and anion
ν+, ν− = number of cations and anions per formula unit
Example – :
Λ°m(NaCl) = λ°(Na+) + λ°(Cl-) = 50.1 + 76.3 = 126.4 S cm2 mol-1
Λ°m = molar conductivity at infinite (zero) dilution.
Each ion contributes independently to total conductivity.
Used to find Λ°m of weak electrolytes (cannot be measured directly).
Λ°m(CH3COOH) = Λ°m(HCl) + Λ°m(CH3COONa) – Λ°m(NaCl)
9
Degree of Dissociation (α) of
Weak Electrolyte
For weak acid (α ≪ 1):
α increases with dilution (Ostwald's dilution law).
At infinite dilution, weak electrolyte is completely dissociated (α = 1).
Λ°m for weak electrolyte obtained by
Kohlrausch's law (not by extrapolation).

Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis
First Law: m = Z·I·t = Z·Q
m = mass deposited; Z = electrochemical equivalent
I = current (A); t = time (s); Q = charge (C)
Second Law: m1/m2 = E1/E2
E = M/n (equivalent mass)
Z = E/F = M/(nF); E = equivalent mass = M/n.
1 F = 96485 C mol-1 ≈ 96500 C mol-1.
Mass deposited ∝ Q (charge passed) [1st law].
Same charge → masses ∝ equivalent masses
[2nd law].

Corrosion
Electrochemical process: iron acts as anode, forms
Fe2+
At anode: Fe → Fe2+ + 2e- (oxidation)
At cathode: + 4 + 4e- → 2 (reduction)
Fe2+ → Fe3+ → Fe2O3·xH2O (rust)
Prevention methods:
Galvanization (Zn coating), cathodic protection, alloying, painting
Corrosion is more rapid in acidic, moist, and saline environments.
Galvanization: Zn (anode) protects Fe (cathode) sacrificially.
Cathodic protection: connect Fe to more active metal (Mg, Zn) or to –ve terminal.
Stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni): layer
prevents further oxidation.

Standard Hydrogen
Electrode (SHE)
Half-cell: H+(aq, 1M) | H2(g, 1atm) | Pt
E°(SHE) = 0.00 V (by convention)
Pt electrode is inert (does not participate in reaction).
SHE is the primary reference electrode.
Calomel electrode used as secondary reference (easier to use than SHE).
20
Equilibrium Constant from E°cell
ΔG° = –nFE° = –RT ln K
log K = nE° / 0.0592 (at 298 K)
K = 10(nE°/0.0592)
Large +ve E° → large K → reaction goes almost to completion.
E° < 0 → K < 1 → reverse reaction is favoured.
At equilibrium: Ecell = 0 (no net current flow).
21
Concentration Cell
Both electrodes of same metal; different ion concentrations
Ecell = (0.0592/n) log (C1/) (at 298 K)
Higher [ion] side → cathode (reduction)
Lower [ion] side → anode (oxidation)
Ecell → 0 as concentrations equalise
Example: Ag | Ag+(0.01M) ‖ Ag+(0.1M) | Ag
Ecell = (0.0592/1) log(0.1/0.01) = 0.0592 V
Used to measure ion concentrations
22
Conductivity of Electrolytes vs
Temperature
Electrolytic solutions: conductivity increases with temperature
Because: viscosity ↓ → ion mobility ↑ → conductivity ↑
Metallic conductors: conductivity decreases with temperature (More collisions of electrons with
vibrating lattice ions)
Molar conductivity vs dilution: Λm ↑ as dilution ↑
Electronic conduction (metals): e- flow;
electrolytic conduction: ion flow.
Electrolytic conductance increases with T (opposite to metals).
Strong electrolytes fully dissociated; Λm rise mainly due to better mobility at dilution.

Electroplating
Electrolytic process to deposit thin layer of metal
Object to be plated = Cathode (–)
Metal to be plated = Anode (+)
Electrolyte = soluble salt of plating metal
Mass deposited: m = (M × I × t) / (n × F)
Cu plating: solution; Cu anode; cathode = object.
Silver plating: solution; Ag anode.
Applications: corrosion prevention, aesthetics, conductivity.
Chrome plating: Cr anode; object is cathode in /.
