6.
Pseudo First Order
Reaction
Condition:
One reactant is in large excess → its concentration is nearly constant
Example (NCERT):
+ → +
True rate law:
Rate = k [CH3COOC2H5][H2O]
Since [H2O] is large and constant:
Rate = k' [ester]
k' = k [H2 O] = constant
Effective order = 1 (pseudo first order)
Another example:
Inversion of cane sugar (sucrose):
+ → +
Actual order = 2
Observed order = 1 (pseudo first order) k' = pseudo first order rate constant
k' = k[H2O]
NCERT: ester hydrolysis is the key example
Water acts as solvent so its conc. is ~55 mol/L and effectively constant

8.
Arrhenius Equation
(Effect of T on k)
Arrhenius Equation: k = A e−Ea/RT
Taking natural log: ln k = ln A −EaRT
Taking log10: log k = log A −Ea2.303 R T
Graph: log k vs 1/T → slope = −E_a / 2.303R
Two-temperature form (NCERT Eq. 4.20):
logk2k1=Ea2.303 R×T2 − T1T1T2
Ea from collision theory: k = P ZAB e−Ea/RT
k = rate constant
A = frequency / Arrhenius factor (pre- exponential factor)
Ea = activation energy
R = 8.314 J/mol K
T = temperature (K)
Higher Ea → slower reaction
A = frequency of collisions with correct orientation
At higher T: more molecules have energy
≥ Ea
9.
Activation Energy (Ea)
Definition (NCERT):
Minimum extra energy required by reactant molecules to transform into products
From Arrhenius equation:
Ea = 2.303 RT1T2T2 − T1× logk2k1
Threshold energy:
Ethreshold = Ea + Ereactants (average)
Energy profile (NCERT Fig. 4.9):
Reactants → Transition state → Products
ΔHrxn = Ea(fwd) − Ea(rev)
Effect of catalyst:
Catalyst lowers Ea → increases k
Ea(cat) < Ea(uncat)
Ea is always positive
Higher Ea: Slower rate More temperature sensitive
Lower Ea: Faster rate Less temp. sensitive Catalyst provides alternate
pathway with lower Ea
Transition state = activated complex (highest energy intermediate)
10.
Collision Theory of Chemical Reactions
Rate equation (NCERT):
Rate = P × ZAB × e−Ea/RT
Collision frequency (Z_AB):
Number of collisions per second per unit volume between A and B molecules
Steric factor (P):
P = Ratio of effective collisions to total collisions
0 < P ≤ 1
Effective collision conditions:
(i) Energy of collision ≥ Ea (energy criterion)
(ii) Proper orientation of molecules (steric criterion)
Fraction of effective collisions:
f = e−Ea/RT
P = steric/probability factor (orientation)
Z_AB = collision frequency
Limitations:
Collision theory fails for complex molecules (P << 1)
Transition state theory overcomes this limitation
NCERT: Rate = PZAB e(−Ea/RT)

14.
Factors Affecting
Rate of Reaction
(a) Concentration (Rate law):
Rate = k [A]x [B]y
Rate ↑ as [reactant] ↑
(b) Temperature (Arrhenius):
k = A e−Ea/RT
Rate ↑ exponentially as T ↑
(c) Catalyst:
Lowers Ea → increases k → increases rate
kcat > kuncat at same T
(d) Surface area (heterogeneous):
Powdered reactant reacts faster than lump
More surface → more collision sites
(e) Nature of reactants:
Ionic > covalent reactions (in solution)
Bond strength, polarity affect rate
NCERT lists 5 factors:
1. Concentration
2. Temperature
3. Catalyst
4. Surface area
5. Nature of reactants
Catalyst:
Does not change ΔH of reaction Only lowers Ea Increases k Light can also
affect some reactions (photochemical)
15.
Mechanism of Reaction (Elementary Steps)
Rate determining step:
The slowest step of a reaction mechanism determines the overall rate of reaction
Example (NCERT):
Overall: 2 + → 2
Mechanism:
Step 1 (slow): + → + F
Step 2 (fast): + F →
Rate = rate of slow step:
Rate = k [NO2][F2]
Order from mechanism:
Order w.r.t. = 1, order w.r.t. = 1
Overall order = 2 (bimolecular RDS)
Key rule:
Rate law written from RDS, not overall eqn.
RDS = rate determining step
Elementary step:
Single step with no intermediates
Intermediate: Formed in one step, consumed in another (not in overall eqn.)
Molecularity of RDS = order of overall reaction
NCERT: rate law must be consistent with mechanism

Radioactive Decay
(First Order Kinetics)
Decay law: N = N0 e−λt
In terms of activity: A = λ N = A0 e−λt
Half-life: t1/2 =0.693λ
Decay constant (λ): λ =2.303tlogN0N
Time for fraction remaining:
t =2.303λlogN0N
Example (NCERT):
: t½ = 1620 years
dating: t½ = 5730 years
N0 = initial no. of radioactive nuclei
N = no. at time t
λ = decay constant
Activity = dN/dt = λN (disintegr./s)
Units of activity:
Curie (Ci): 1 Ci = 3.7×1010 disintegrations/s
Becquerel (Bq): 1 Bq = 1 disint./s
Radioactive decay: Always first order (NCERT)
