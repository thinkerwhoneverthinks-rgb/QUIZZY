d-Block: Definition &
Position
Elements in which last e– enters (n-1)d orbitals
Groups 3 to 12 in the Periodic Table
Four series:
3d series: Sc (21) → Zn (30)
4d series: Y (39) → Cd (48)
5d series: La (57)/Hf (72) → Hg (80)
6d series: Ac (89)/Rf (104) → Cn (112)
Called Transition Elements (incomplete d-subshell in atom or ion)
Zn, Cd, Hg: d10s2 — NOT true transition elements (complete d-shell, no variable valence/colour)
La and Ac: technically belong to f-block but placed in 5d/6d for continuity
Bridge between active s-block metals and less active p-block elements

Atomic & Ionic Radii
Atomic radii generally decrease from left to right across a period
But the decrease is small (increased nuclear charge offset by d-d shielding)
3d series (pm):
Sc=161, Ti=145, V=132, Cr=125, Mn=124, Fe=124,
Co=125, Ni=125, Cu=128, Zn=133
Ionic radii decrease with increasing oxidation state:
Mn2+ > Mn3+ > Mn4+
5d series metals (W, Pt, Au) almost same radius as
4d (Mo, Pd, Ag) → Lanthanoid Contraction
Lanthanoid contraction: steady decrease in atomic/ionic size across lanthanoid series
Consequence: 4d and 5d elements in same group have very similar properties
e.g., Zr & Hf, Nb & Ta, Mo & W nearly identical in size
4
Ionisation Enthalpies
Generally increase across a period (left to right) BUT irregular due to extra stability of
d5 and d10
1st IE (kJ/mol) of 3d series (approx):
Sc=633, Ti=659, V=650, Cr=653, Mn=717, Fe=762, Co=760, Ni=737, Cu=745, Zn=906
Successive IE:
ΔiH1 < ΔiH2 < ΔiH3 (each successive IE larger)
Mn has higher IE than Cr (d5 is extra stable; removing 1 e– from Mn half-filled d5 is harder)
IE values lie between s-block (low) and p-block (high) elements
All are relatively high IE → metals are less reactive than alkali metals
High IE (Pt, Au) → used in jewellery (resistant to corrosion)

Lanthanoid Contraction
Steady decrease in atomic/ionic radii from La to Lu
Ionic radii (Ln3+) in pm:
La=106, Ce=103, Pr=101, Nd=99, Pm=98, Sm=96,
Eu=95, Gd=94, Tb=92, Dy=91, Ho=89, Er=88,
Tm=87, Yb=86, Lu=85
Total contraction from La3+ to Lu3+ ≈ 21 pm
Cause: 4f electrons have poor shielding of nuclear charge for each other (penetration into inner shells
is low)
Each successive element adds one 4f electron
→ nuclear charge increases → electrons pulled inward
Consequences:
(i) 4d and 5d elements have similar sizes (in same group) e.g., Zr ≈ Hf
(ii) Properties of 4d and 5d elements closely resemble each other → difficult to separate
(iii) Basic strength of Ln(OH)3 decreases from to
(iv) Gradual increase in electronegativity across series

Oxidation States of Lanthanoids
Most common: +3 (Ln3+) — very stable
Some show +2 and +4:
+4: Ce4+ (extra stable: f0 configuration after losing
4e−)
Pr4+, Tb4+ (less stable)
+2: Eu2+ (extra stable: f7 half-filled after losing 2e−)
Sm2+, Yb2+ (less stable)
All lanthanoids: predominantly ionic compounds
salts are good oxidising agents (Ce4+ → Ce3+)
+3 oxidation state: removing 2 × 6s + 1 × 5d or 4f electron
Extra stability of empty, half-filled, and fully-filled f orbitals drives +4/+2 oxidation states
Ce4+ used as an oxidising agent in volumetric analysis (cerimetry)
Eu2+ used in luminescence/phosphorescence applications
Wide variety of +2, +3 and +4 compounds, unlike actinoids, which show many more oxidation states

Magnetic & Spectral
Properties of Lanthanoids
Magnetic moments: most lanthanoids are paramagnetic
μ depends on both spin and orbital motion (L-S coupling important)
Approximate μ (BM):
Ce3+(f1): 2.54 Pr3+(f2): 3.58
Nd3+(f3): 3.62 Sm3+(f5): 1.55
Gd3+(f7): 7.94 Dy3+(f9): 10.65
Er3+(f11): 9.58 Yb3+(f13): 4.54
La3+(f0) and Lu3+(f14): diamagnetic
Spectral lines: narrow and sharp (4f electrons shielded by 5s25p6)
Unlike d-block ions, orbital contribution cannot be ignored for lanthanoid μ
4f electrons are inner electrons → shielded from ligand field → ligand field splitting very small
Colours of Ln3+ ions (in solution) are faint and arise from f-f transitions
f-f transitions: Laporte forbidden → low intensity absorption
Gd3+ (f7): maximum μ = 7.94 BM (7 unpaired f electrons)
