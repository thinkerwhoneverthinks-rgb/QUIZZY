4
Mole Fraction (χ)
For binary solution (A = solvent , B = solute):
χA =nAnA + nB
χB =nBnA + nB
χA + χB = 1
For electrolyte solution:
χB =i nBnA + i nB
nA = moles of solvent
nB = moles of solute
i = van't Hoff factor
Mole fraction is dimensionless
Sum of all χ = 1
Temperature independent
Used directly in Raoult's law
5
Molarity (M)
M =Moles of soluteVolume of solution (L)
In terms of mass: M =WB ×1000MB × V (mL)
Dilution law: M1V1 = M2V2
Relation to normality: N = M × n −factor
WB = mass of solute (g)
MB = molar mass (g/mol)
V = volume in mL or L
Units: mol L–1 or M
Temperature Dependent (volume changes with T)
NCERT: most widely used lab unit
6
Molality (m)
m =Moles of soluteMass of solvent (kg)
In terms of mass: m =WB ×1000MB × WA
Relation with molarity (M):
m =1000 × M1000 × dsoln − M × MB
WA = mass of solvent (g)
WB = mass of solute (g)
MB = molar mass (g/mol)
d = density (g/mL)
Units: mol kg–1
Temperature INDEPENDENT
(mass doesn't change with T)
Preferred for colligative properties
7
Henry's Law
Statement: The partial pressure of a gas in vapour phase is proportional to its mole
fraction in the solution.
p = KH × χ
p = partial pressure of gas
χ = mole fraction of gas in solution
KH = Henry's law constant
Effect of temperature:
∴ Solubility of gas ↓ as T ↑
Higher KH → lower solubility
KH at 298 K:
He: 144.97 kbar
: 74.84 kbar
: 76.48 kbar
: 46.82 kbar
: 1.67 kbar
Applications:
Aerated drinks
Scuba diving
Dissolved for fish

Raoult's Law (Volatile solute / Liquid–Liquid
solutions)
For each volatile component: pi = χi × pi°
pi = partial vapour pressure of component i
χi = mole fraction of i
p°i = V.P. of pure i
NCERT Statement:
Partial V.P. of each volatile component ∝ its mole fraction
ptotal vs χA gives straight line (ideal)
Valid for ideal solutions only

(Non-volatile solute)
Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure
(RLVP)
V.P. of solution: ps = χsolvent × p°
RLVP:
p° − psp°= χsolute = χB
In terms of moles:p° − psp°=nBnA + nB
For very dilute solution:p° − psp°≈nBnA
Molar mass of solute (M2):
M2 = w2 × p° × M1w1(p° − ps)
p° = V.P. of pure solvent
ps = V.P. of solution
nA = moles of solvent
nB = moles of solute
w1,M1 = mass & MM of solvent
w2,M2 = mass & MM of solute
1st Colligative property Depends only on number of solute particles, not nature

Ideal & Non-ideal
Solutions
Ideal Solution: Obeys Raoult's law at all concentrations
ΔHmix = 0
ΔVmix = 0
A−B ≈ A−A ≈ B−B interactions
e.g., benzene–toluene, hexane–heptane
Non-ideal: Positive deviation:
ptotal > Raoult's law value
ΔHmix > 0 (endothermic)
ΔVmix > 0 (expansion)
e.g., ethanol–acetone, –toluene
Non-ideal: Negative deviation:
ptotal < Raoult's law value
ΔHmix < 0 (exothermic)
ΔVmix < 0 (contraction)
A−B < A−A or interaction
A−B > A−A or interaction
e.g., phenol–aniline, –acetone
Azeotropes: constant boiling mixtures
Min. boiling azeotrope: Positive deviation
e.g., EtOH–
(95.4% EtOH, 78.1°C)
Max. boiling azeotrope:
Negative deviation
e.g., –
(68% , 393.5 K)

Van't Hoff Factor (i)
Definition:
Observed colligative property
i = Calculated (normal) colligative property
i = Total moles of particles in solution
Moles of solute dissolved
Modified equations:
ΔTb = i × Kb × m
ΔTf = i × Kf × m
π = i C R T
p° − psp°= i × χB
Degree of dissociation (α): α =i −1n −1
Degree of association (α): α =1 − i1 −1 / n
For electrolytes: i > 1
(dissociation → more particles)
Non-electrolytes: i = 1
Association: i < 1
e.g., in benzene (dimers, i ≈ 0.5)
n = ions per formula unit (dissociation)
n = molecules per associate (association)

i > 1 ⇒ Mobs < Mnormal

Solubility of Gases
(Effect of T and P)
Henry's Law:
p = KH × χ
Solubility ∝ partial pressure
Effect of Temperature:
Dissolution of gas → exothermic
KH ↑ as T ↑ ⇒ Solubility ↓
(Le Chatelier's principle)
Effect of Pressure:
Solubility ∝ partial pressure (P)
Increasing P → more gas dissolves
KH at 298 K:
He: 144.97 kbar
: 74.84 kbar
: 76.48 kbar
: 46.82 kbar
: 1.67 kbar
Lower KH → more soluble in water
Thermal pollution:
Warm water has less → harms aquatic life
