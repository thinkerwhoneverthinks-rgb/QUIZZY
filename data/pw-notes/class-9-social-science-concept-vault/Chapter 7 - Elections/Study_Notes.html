Chapter 7: Elections — Complete Short Notes

Why Do Elections Matter?

Meaning and Importance of Elections

Elections = core democratic method where citizens use their rights to vote and choose representatives + governments.
  • Regular + periodic elections are the backbone of democracy.
  • Elections let citizens:
    • Choose representatives
    • Take part in government formation
    • Hold representatives accountable
    • Remove non-performing representatives
    • Give legitimacy to government (authority comes from people’s consent)
    • Secure political equality (each eligible vote has equal value)
    • Ensure representation of people + interests
  • An election is both:
    • A process where citizens exercise the right to vote
    • A civic responsibility (citizens’ duty/role in democratic participation)
  • Fresh mandate principle: reps can’t stay in office forever without a new public mandate via elections.
  • Genuine elections need real choice among candidates/parties; no real alternatives = not truly democratic.
  • The vote is the main tool to both choose reps and hold them accountable.

Direct and Indirect Elections

Direct election: citizens vote directly to elect their representatives. Indirect election: citizens elect representatives, and those reps elect another office-holder.
India: direct elections (every five years)
  • Lok Sabha
  • Vidhan Sabha (State Legislative Assembly)
  • Panchayats
  • Municipal corporations and other local bodies
India: indirect elections
  • President of India
  • Vice-President of India
  • Members of the Rajya Sabha
  • Many members of State Legislative Councils

Major Democratic Values Promoted by Elections

  • Accountability: reps must answer to citizens + can be removed.
  • Representation: different groups/interests get a voice in governance.
  • Legitimacy: government authority comes from consent of the people.
  • Equality: every eligible citizen has an equal vote.
  • Participation: citizens join public decision-making by voting.
  • Choice: voters pick among competing candidates/parties/policies.

Psephology

Psephology = scientific study of elections.
  • Word origin:
    • Greek “psēphos” = pebble
    • “logy” = systematic study
  • Historical basis: Ancient Greeks used pebbles for voting → hence the term.

Inside India’s Election Machinery

Need for an Organised Electoral System

For elections to truly support democracy, they must be:
  • Free (voters choose without coercion)
  • Fair (no undue advantage; equal chance to compete)
  • Transparent (process + results open to scrutiny)
  • Inclusive (all eligible citizens can participate)
  • Impartial (neutral administration)
  • Trustworthy (public confidence in the process)
  • In a big + diverse country, running elections needs:
    • Laws
    • National, state and local coordination
    • Administrative machinery (officials, systems, logistics)
    • Security arrangements
    • Technology
    • Public awareness

Major Stakeholders

Stakeholders = main participants whose roles/actions decide how elections are conducted + how credible they are.
  • Key stakeholders in India’s elections:
    • Election Commission of India
    • Political parties
    • Candidates
    • Civil society
    • Media
    • Voters
    • Government officials
    • Security personnel
  • The Constitution + parliamentary laws lay down the functions and powers of these stakeholders.

The Electoral System

Meaning of an Electoral System

An electoral system tells us how votes turn into seats in the legislature, and how winners are picked.
  • Electoral systems can differ in:
    • Seat allocation (how many seats each party/candidate gets)
    • Winner selection rules (how a winner is declared)
  • India’s Constitution-makers looked at different options and chose the First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) system for Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha elections.

Systems Used in India

FPTP is used for:
  • Lok Sabha
  • Vidhan Sabha
Proportional Representation is used for:
  • Rajya Sabha
  • President
  • Vice-President
  • Vidhan Parishad elections

First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) System

FPTP = the candidate with the highest votes in a constituency wins, even if they don’t get an absolute majority (>50%).
  • Constituencies: the country/state is divided into constituencies.
  • Single-member constituency: each constituency elects one representative.
  • One voter → one vote → one candidate: voter chooses one candidate.
  • Winning rule: the candidate with the greatest number of votes wins.
  • Not necessary to secure more than 50% of total votes.
Worked example (why majority isn’t needed in FPTP):
  • Party A: 40%
  • Party B: 35%
  • Party C: 25%
Result: Party A wins (largest vote share), even though 60% voted against it (35% + 25%).
  • Features of FPTP:
    • Simple to understand + run.
    • Gives a clear constituency representative (direct area–member link).
    • Often leads to stable governments (usually favours bigger parties).
    • Limitation: winner may win without majority support.
    • Disproportionality: vote share and seat share can be very different.
Exam trap: Don’t mix up “highest votes” (plurality = FPTP) with “more than 50%” (majority). In FPTP, you only need the biggest share, not an absolute majority.

Majority System

A majority system needs the winner to get more than 50% votes.
  • Each voter chooses one candidate.
  • Majority requirement: candidate must get > 50%.
  • If nobody gets a majority in the first round, the top two face off in a second round.
Worked example (two-round runoff logic):
  • First round: Party X = 45%, Party Y = 40%, Party Z = 15%
  • No one has >50%, so X and Y go to round 2.
  • If Party Y gets 55% in round 2, Party Y wins (now a majority).

Proportional Representation (PR)

Proportional Representation (PR) = seats match vote share (usually people vote for a party, not an individual).
  • Voters generally vote for a political party.
  • Seats are given to parties in proportion to their vote share.
Worked example (seats proportional to votes): In a 100-seat legislature:
  • Party M: 40% votes → 40 seats
  • Party N: 35% votes → 35 seats
  • Party O: 15% votes → 15 seats
  • Party P: 10% votes → 10 seats
  • Features of PR:
    • Seat share closely reflects vote share.
    • Smaller groups get representation.
    • Power sharing according to support.
    • Can encourage coalition governments.

State Legislatures

Vidhan Sabha

Vidhan Sabha = directly elected Legislative Assembly of a state (and some Union Territories).
  • Makes laws for the state (and certain Union Territories).
  • Members are elected by FPTP, like Lok Sabha.

Bicameral State Legislatures

Bicameral state legislature = a state has two houses: lower + upper.
States with two houses (as listed):
  • Andhra Pradesh
  • Bihar
  • Karnataka
  • Maharashtra
  • Telangana
  • Uttar Pradesh
Houses:
  • Vidhan Sabha = Lower House / Legislative Assembly
  • Vidhan Parishad = Upper House / Legislative Council

Composition of the Vidhan Parishad

Vidhan Parishad = upper house in a bicameral state; members come via different electorates + nominations.
  • Members are chosen by:
    • Members of the Vidhan Sabha
    • Local bodies
    • Registered graduates
    • Teachers
    • Governor’s nominations
  • Governor’s nominations are for people distinguished in:
    • Art
    • Science
    • Literature
    • Social service
    • Cooperatives
  • Vidhan Parishad elections use Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (STV).

Single Transferable Vote (STV)

Calculation of Quota

Quota = (Total valid votes / (Seats to be filled + 1)) + 1 Total valid votes = number of valid (non-invalid) votes cast Seats to be filled = number of vacancies to be elected Quota = minimum number of votes required for election

Counting and Transfer Process

In STV, voters rank candidates; votes are counted + transferred so seats get filled and representation stays proportional.
  1. Preference ranking: voters rank candidates in order of preference.
  2. First-preference count: count all first-preference votes.
  3. Quota check: anyone who reaches the quota is declared elected.
  4. Elimination: the lowest-ranked candidate is eliminated.
  5. Transfer: eliminated candidate’s votes move to remaining candidates using the next preference on each ballot.
  6. Repeat: keep counting + transferring until all seats are filled.
Exam tip: Always link STV with proportional representation + its use here: Vidhan Parishad elections.

Election Laws

Representation of the People Act, 1950

RPA, 1950: sets the base framework for constituencies + voters (seats, delimitation, electoral rolls, voting rights).
  • Primarily deals with:
    • Allocation of legislative seats
    • Delimitation of constituencies
    • Preparation of electoral rolls
    • Revision of electoral rolls
    • Voting rights of citizens aged 18 or above
    • Prevention of discrimination in voter registration

Representation of the People Act, 1951

RPA, 1951: handles the actual conduct of elections—nominations, campaigning, voting procedure, offences, disputes + their resolution.
  • Regulates:
    • Conduct of elections
    • Candidate nominations
    • Election campaigns
    • Voting procedures
    • Electoral offences
    • Corrupt practices
    • Post-election disputes
    • Resolution of election-related conflicts
Together, the Acts (1950 + 1951) promote:
  • Integrity
  • Transparency
  • Accountability
  • Fairness

Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952

Presidential & Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952: regulates elections to the offices of President and Vice-President.

Electoral Offences and Corrupt Practices

Bribery and Inducement

Bribery/inducement: when a candidate (or someone acting with the candidate’s consent) offers gifts/promises/benefits to influence electoral behaviour.
  • A candidate, or another person acting with the candidate’s consent, cannot offer gifts/promises/benefits to persuade someone to:
    • Contest an election
    • Withdraw from an election
    • Vote
    • Refrain from voting

Appeals to Identity

Appeals to identity: banned—candidates can’t seek votes (or ask people not to vote) by invoking certain identity categories.
  • Candidates cannot ask people to vote or not vote on the basis of:
    • Religion
    • Caste
    • Race
    • Community
    • Language

Misuse of Government Personnel

Misuse of government personnel: improper use of official staff/administrative machinery to benefit a candidate/party during elections.

Restrictions on Election Assistance from Government-Related Personnel

Rule (candidate assistance restriction): candidates cannot take election assistance from certain government-related personnel—this would affect neutrality + fairness of the process.
  • Gazetted officers
  • Judges and magistrates
  • Armed forces personnel
  • Police personnel
  • Excise officers
Exam tip: List these categories exactly as given. Questions often ask “who cannot assist candidates” to test administrative neutrality.

Delimitation Commission

Meaning of Delimitation

Delimitation: (1) deciding number of seats, (2) fixing territorial boundaries of constituencies, and (3) adjusting Lok Sabha + Vidhan Sabha constituencies as per population.
  • Constituency boundaries are not permanently fixed.
  • Population changes can make constituencies unequal over time.
  • Without delimitation → unequal representation (e.g., one representative for 5 lakh vs another for 25 lakh).
Core idea: delimitation keeps constituencies roughly similar in population so each vote has broadly similar value.

Purpose of Delimitation

Purpose: fair + equal representation by keeping constituencies reasonably comparable in population, and keeping the population:seat ratio approx equal.
  • Constituencies have reasonably comparable populations.
  • Population : seats ratio is approximately equal.
  • Citizens get fair and equal representation.

Constitutional Basis of Delimitation

Article 82: provides for setting up a Delimitation Commission.
Delimitation Commissions (India): 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002.
Exam tip: Learn the 4 years in order. Also: note the long gap (1973 → 2002)—often asked as a short-answer “why/identify the interval” type prompt.

Election Commission of India (ECI)

Constitutional Status

ECI: an autonomous, permanent, constitutional body that administers elections in India.
  • Set up on 25 January 1950.
  • Articles 324–329: election administration + constitutional powers/duties about elections.
  • Constitution gives ECI superintendence, direction, and control of the electoral process.
Must-quote phrase: “superintendence, direction and control” of elections.

Elections Conducted by the ECI

Scope: ECI conducts elections at national/state level + key constitutional offices.
  • Lok Sabha
  • Rajya Sabha
  • Vidhan Sabha
  • Vidhan Parishad
  • President
  • Vice-President
Common mistake: don’t forget Rajya Sabha and Vidhan Parishad in the list.

Election Administration at State and Local Levels

State-level supervision: each state has a Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) to supervise election work.
  • The ECI appoints the CEO from among civil servants after consulting the state government.
  • Other officials given election duties include:
  • District Magistrates
  • Sub-Divisional Magistrates
  • Tahsildars
  • District Election Officers
  • Returning Officers
  • Electoral Registration Officers
Exam focus: election machinery is multi-tiered—know the CEO (state) + the district/sub-district officer set that executes election tasks.

Functions of the ECI

Preparation of Electoral Rolls

Electoral roll: official list of eligible voters in a constituency — only names on the roll can vote.
  • Official enumerators collect details of eligible voters.
  • Separate roll for each constituency.
  • Roll is arranged polling-station-wise (for admin convenience).
Common mistake: Don’t write “any citizen can vote.” Correct: a citizen can vote only if eligible and their name is on the electoral roll.

Special Intensive Revision (SIR)

Special Intensive Revision (SIR): process to update + verify + correct electoral rolls (accuracy + inclusiveness).
Objectives of SIR (learn as points):
  • Include every eligible citizen
  • Add young citizens who have turned 18
  • Prevent inclusion of ineligible people
  • Correct inaccurate entries
  • Remove duplicate registrations
  • Remove names of deceased voters
  • Update voters who changed residence
  • Delete permanently untraceable voters
SIR workflow (ECI steps):
  1. Publishes a revised roll
  2. Allows citizens to file claims and objections
  3. Settles disputes
  4. Publishes the final electoral roll
Exam tip: For “how rolls are corrected,” write both: (1) objectives (what gets corrected) + (2) procedure (publish → claims/objections → settle → final publication).

Voting by Service Voters: ETPBS

ETPBS: Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System.
  • Mainly for service voters.
  • Voters posted away from home constituency can receive postal ballot electronically.
  • They cast + return vote from their place of posting.
Who may be service voters (as listed):
  • Armed forces personnel
  • Members of certain armed police forces serving outside their state
  • Government personnel serving abroad
  • Spouses covered by applicable electoral provisions
Common mistake: ETPBS ≠ “electronic voting.” It’s a postal ballot sent electronically (not online EVM voting).

Election Scheduling

Deciding Election Dates

When ECI activates election machinery: (1) when a legislature completes its 5-year term, or (2) when it is dissolved before completing the term.
Factors for the election schedule:
  • Weather
  • Agricultural cycles
  • School & university examinations
  • Festivals
  • Security
  • Geography & accessibility
  • Administrative requirements
Exam tip: For “why elections in phases / why dates vary,” justify using these factors.

Party registration: parties must register with ECI to contest as registered parties.
  • Candidates can also contest as independents (no party needed).

Inner-Party Democracy

Free, Fair and Inclusive Elections

Meaning of Electoral Fairness

Electoral fairness: just holding elections isn’t enough; democracy suffers if the process is compromised or people can’t participate meaningfully.
Elections lose democratic value if:

  • Votes are inaccurately counted.
  • Eligible people can’t reach/use polling facilities.
  • The process is biased.
  • Citizens lose confidence in electoral institutions.
Qualities of a democratic election (must list):

Multi-party system: system where many political parties compete to gain political power.

Relationship Between Citizens, Parties and Government

Know the sequence from society → government action:
  1. Citizens build public opinion.
  2. Political parties organise/shape that opinion.
  3. Parties fight elections via candidates + manifestos.
  4. Winning party or coalition forms the government.
  5. Government makes + implements policies.
  6. Opposition parties join debates + committees.

Defection and Anti-Defection Law

Meaning of Defection

Defection: when an elected member leaves/abandons the party on whose ticket they were elected.
  • Forms defection may involve:
    • Joining another party
    • Acting independently
    • Voting against party instructions

Differing Views

  • Criticism: seen as political opportunism → weakens party discipline + government stability.
  • Defence: can be defended as conscience or responding to changing public expectations.

Anti-Defection Law

Anti-Defection Law: brought in 1985 via the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act to stop politically motivated defections and strengthen government stability.
Grounds for disqualification of a legislator:
  • Voluntarily giving up party membership
  • Voting against the party whip
  • Abstaining against party instructions (as per applicable rules)
Who decides disqualification? the Speaker (House) or the Chairman (House), as applicable.
Don’t write only “switching parties”. Also add: voting against the whip + abstaining against instructions (common exam triggers).

Recognition of Political Parties

National Party Recognition

National Party: recognised if it meets any one condition below:
  1. At least 6% valid votes in Lok Sabha/Assembly elections in 4+ states and wins at least 4 Lok Sabha seats.
  2. Wins at least 2% of Lok Sabha seats, with winners from at least 3 states.
  3. Recognised as a state party in at least 4 states.
Connector trap: Condition 1 is 6% in 4+ states AND 4 Lok Sabha seats. Conditions 2 & 3 are separate alternatives.

State Party Recognition

State Party (in a state): recognised if it meets any one condition below:
  1. At least 6% valid Assembly votes + wins at least 2 Assembly seats.
  2. At least 6% Lok Sabha votes in that state + wins at least 1 Lok Sabha seat.
  3. Wins at least 3% of Assembly seats or at least 3 seats, whichever is greater.
  4. Wins at least 1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 Lok Sabha seats (or fraction) allotted to the state.
  5. Secures at least 8% valid votes in the state in a Lok Sabha/Assembly election.
Condition 3 is a classic trap: 3% OR 3 seats, whichever is greater (not smaller).

Registered Unrecognised Political Parties

Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPPs): registered parties that:
  • haven’t met the required vote % or seats for recognition, or
  • may never have contested an election after registration.
For factual Qs, use ECI information as the official reference (for counting state parties + RUPPs).

Development of Alliance Politics

  • 1977: parties came together as the Janata Party after the National Emergency (1975–77).
  • Janata Government: the first national-level coalition government.
  • Late 1990s onwards: major national alliances:
    • National Democratic Alliance (NDA) — led by BJP

Important Election Technologies

This heading starts the next section: when you continue, carry forward the tech already introduced here—EVM, VVPAT, cVIGIL, and technology/e-governance.

Electronic Voting Machine (EVM)

Electronic Voting Machine (EVM): device that electronically records votes during elections.
  • EVMs help rapid + organised voting and make counting faster & more systematic.
  • Braille-enabled EVMs improve accessibility for voters with visual impairment.
Exam link: EVMs → efficiency (speed/organisation) + inclusion (Braille-enabled access).

Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT)

VVPAT: system that shows a temporary paper record of the chosen candidate so the voter can verify correct recording.
  • Displays a paper slip/record briefly with the selected candidate.
  • Enables voter verification (recorded choice = intended choice).
  • Creates a paper audit mechanism (physical record for audit/verification if needed).
Common exam slip: Don’t write “VVPAT is for showing results.” Main purpose = voter verification + audit trail.

Inclusion of Different Groups in Elections

Groups for which electoral arrangements should be studied

  • Persons with Disabilities (PwDs)
  • Service voters
  • Senior citizens
  • Prisoners
  • Persons in preventive detention

Specific inclusion measures identified

  • Saksham + accessible facilities for PwDs (aim: equal participation).
  • ETPBS for service voters.
  • Home voting for eligible citizens above 85 and persons with benchmark disability.
  • Braille EVMs + wheelchairs as polling accessibility support.
Q: “How are elections made inclusive?” Quote at least 3 measures: Braille EVMs/wheelchairs, ETPBS, home voting (85+ & benchmark disability).

Voting Without Voter ID

Voting without a voter ID card: if your name is on the electoral roll, you can vote using another ECI-approved ID when the voter card isn’t available.
  • Eligibility depends on electoral roll entry, not just having the voter card.
  • Alt IDs can include passport, driving licence, or another notified photo ID approved by ECI.
Exam trap: No voter ID card ≠ no vote. Condition: name on roll + ECI-approved ID.

Model Code of Conduct (MCC): Common Violations

Model Code of Conduct (MCC): behaviour rules for parties/candidates to keep elections fair; chapter flags common violations via a case study.
  • Wall writing.
  • Posters pasted without permission.
  • Prohibited campaigning close to polling time.
  • Campaigning during the silence period.
  • Unauthorised slogans/publicity.
MCC answers: write specific acts (wall writing, unauthorised posters/slogans) + timing rules (near polling time; silence period breaches).

Voting by Police and Armed Forces

Service voters (incl. eligible police/armed forces in service contexts) can use special arrangements like postal ballots or ETPBS.
  • Eligible service voters: vote via postal ballots or ETPBS.
  • Election-duty personnel: vote using election-duty certificates or via postal-ballot arrangements as per rules.
Don’t mix categories: “Service voters” vs “election-duty personnel” may have different provisions (postal/ETPBS vs duty certificates/postal ballots). Mention both.

Voting Rights and Party Systems

Multi-party democracy

Multi-party democracy: many parties compete → voters get meaningful competition + policy choice.

Single-party system

Single-party system: voting may exist, but political choice is limited because competition is restricted (dominance/no real alternatives).

Monarchy without voting rights or political parties

Monarchy without voting rights/parties: citizens have no electoral participation due to absence of voting rights + political parties.
In comparisons, use the chapter’s exact contrasts: meaningful competition & policy choice (multi-party) vs limited choice despite voting (single-party) vs no electoral participation (monarchy without voting/parties).

  • Periodic elections ensure:
