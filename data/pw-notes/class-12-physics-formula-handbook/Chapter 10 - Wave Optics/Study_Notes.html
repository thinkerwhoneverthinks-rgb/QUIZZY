Wave Optics

S. No.ConceptFormulaOther Information
1WavefrontN/AThe continuous locus of all particles of a medium vibrating in the same phase. Light travels in the form of these wavefronts.
2Types of WavefrontsPoint source → SphericalLine source → CylindricalSource at infinity → PlaneRays of light (wave normals) are always drawn perpendicular to the wavefront at any given point.
3Huygens’ PrincipleEvery point on a primary wavefront acts as a fresh source of secondary wavelets, spreading out at the speed of light in that medium.
4Refraction via HuygensProves Snell’s law using the time taken by secondary wavelets to travel between two wavefronts in different media.
5Reflection via HuygensDerived by showing that triangles formed by the incident and reflected wavefronts are congruent.
6Coherent SourcesTwo sources emitting light waves of the same frequency, wavelength, and having a constant (or zero) phase difference.
7Superposition PrincipleWhen two or more waves travel through a medium, the net displacement is the vector sum of individual displacements.
8Phase & Path DifferenceΔϕ is the phase difference in radians. Δx is the path difference. Crucial for determining interference conditions.
9Interference AmplitudeThe resultant amplitude when two coherent waves of amplitudes and superimpose.
10Interference IntensityDerived from . The term is the interference term dictating energy redistribution.
11Constructive InterferenceWhere . Condition for maxima (bright fringes). Waves meet exactly in phase.
12Maximum IntensityOccurs at points of constructive interference.Energy is conserved but localized.
13Destructive InterferenceWhere n = 1, 2, 3 . . . Condition for minima (dark fringes). Waves meet exactly out of phase.
14Minimum IntensityOccurs at points of destructive interference.Complete darkness only if both sources have equal intensities.
15Intensity Ratio are slit widths. Intensity is directly proportional to the width of the slits.
16YDSE Path Differencey = distance on screen, d = slit separation, D = slit-to-screen distance. Valid for D >> d.
17Bright Fringes (YDSE)Distance of the nth bright fringe from the central maximum. n = 0 corresponds to the central maximum.
18Dark Fringes (YDSE)Distance of the nth dark fringe from the central maximum. n = 1 is the first minimum.
19Fringe Width (β)The distance between two consecutive bright or two consecutive dark fringes. In YDSE, all fringes are of equal width.
20Angular Fringe Width (θ)The angle subtended by one fringe width at the center of the slits. Independent of the screen distance D.
21Single Slit Diffraction (Path Difference)a is the width of the single slit. θ is the angle of diffraction for a point P on the screen.
22Minima in DiffractionWhere n = 1, 2, 3 . . . Gives the position of secondary minima (dark bands). Note the formula difference from YDSE.
23Maxima in DiffractionWhere n = 1, 2, 3 . . . Gives the position of secondary maxima. Intensity falls off rapidly with increasing n.
24Central Maximum WidthThe central maximum is distinctively twice as wide as any of the secondary maxima and contains the vast majority of the intensity.
25Interference vs. DiffractionIntensity DistributionInterference fringes have uniform intensity and width. Diffraction fringes vary in width (central is double) and intensity rapidly decreases.
