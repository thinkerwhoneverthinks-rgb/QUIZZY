Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

S. No.ConceptFormulaOther Information
1Electric Potential ()Work done by an external force in bringing a unit positive charge from infinity to a point. Scalar quantity. Unit: Volt (V) or J/C.
2Potential due to a Point Charge.Potential is positive for a positive charge and negative for a negative charge. Zero at infinity.
3Potential due to System of ChargesSuperposition principle applies. Algebraic sum of individual potentials (signs of charges must be taken into account).
4Electric Dipole Moment Vector directed from negative charge to positive charge. 2a is the distance between the charges.Unit: C·m.
5Potential due to a Dipole (Axial Line)For short dipolePotential drops off as 1/r2, which is faster than the 1/r drop-off of a single point charge.
6Potential due to a Dipole (Equatorial Line)Any point on the equatorial plane is equidistant from both +q and −q. The work done in moving a charge along this plane is zero.
7Potential due to a Dipole (General Point)A surface with a constant value of potential at all points. No work is required to move a test charge on this surface.
8Equipotential SurfacesA surface with a constant value of potential at all points. No work is required to move a test charge on this surface.
9Properties of Equipotential SurfacesElectric field is always normal to the equipotential surface at every point. Two equipotential surfaces can never intersect.
10Relation between and Electric field is in the direction in which the potential decreases steepest. Its magnitude is given by the change in potential per unit displacement.
11Potential Energy (U) (Two Point Charges)Work done in assembling the system of charges by bringing them from infinity to their present locations.
12Potential Energy (U) (In External Field)Total energy of a system of two charges in an external electric field. V(r) is the external potential at the location of the charge.
13Work Done in Rotating a DipoleWork done by an external torque to rotate a dipole from angle to in a uniform electric field.
14Potential Energy of DipoleStable equilibrium: θ = 0° (U = −pE).Unstable equilibrium: θ = 180° (U = +pE).
15Electrostatics of ConductorsThe interior of a conductor can have no excess charge. The entire conductor is an equipotential volume.
16Electrostatic ShieldingWhatever be the charge and field configuration outside, any cavity in a conductor remains shielded from outside electric influence.
17Dielectrics and PolarizationNon-conducting substances. In an external field, dipole moments are induced. P is polarization density, is electric susceptibility.
18Electrical Capacitance (C)Measure of the ability of a conductor to store charge. Depends purely on geometry and the medium. Unit: Farad (F).
