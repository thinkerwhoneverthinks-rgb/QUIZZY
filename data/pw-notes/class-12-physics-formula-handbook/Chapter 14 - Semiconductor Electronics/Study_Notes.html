S. No.ConceptFormulaOther Information
1Energy Bands in SolidsN/AIn a crystal, discrete energy levels of individual atoms split to form continuous energy bands due to interatomic interactions.
2Valence & Conduction BandsN/AValence Band (VB): Highest energy band containing electrons.Conduction Band (CB): Lowest empty or partially filled band.
3Energy Band Gap ()Energy required to move an electron from the top of the VB to the bottom of the CB. No electron can exist in this gap.
4Classification by At absolute zero, semiconductors behave as perfect insulators because the conduction band is completely empty.
5Types of SemiconductorsElemental: Si, GeCompound: GaAs, CdS, AnthraceneElemental are widely used in core electronics;compounds are often used in optoelectronics (though primarily elemental is tested in CBSE).
6Intrinsic SemiconductorsPure semiconductors. Number of free electrons () strictly equals the number of holes (). is intrinsic carrier concentration.
7Thermal GenerationAs temperature increases, thermal energy breaks covalent bonds, generating electron-hole pairs and increasing conductivity
8Doping & Extrinsic SemiconductorsN/ADeliberate addition of desirable impurities (ppm level) to increase conductivity. The impurity atoms are called dopants
9n-type SemiconductorDoped with Pentavalent impurity (P, As, Sb). Four electrons bond, the fifth becomes a free charge carrier. is donor concentration.
10p-type SemiconductorDoped with Trivalent impurity (B, Al, In). Creates a vacancy (hole) that can accept an electron. is acceptor concentration.
11Mass Action LawIn thermal equilibrium, the product of majority and minority carrier concentrations is constant and independent of the doping level.
12Donor Energy Level () (n-type)Created just below the conduction band. Very little thermal energy is needed to excite donor electrons into the CB.
13Acceptor Energy Level () (p-type)Created just above the valence band. Electrons from VB easily jump here, leaving behind holes in the VB.
14Electrical Conductivity (σ) are drift mobilities. Since , an n-type semiconductor has slightly higher conductivity than a p-type for the same doping level.
15p-n Junction: DiffusionDue to concentration gradient, holes diffuse from p to n, and electrons diffuse from n to p.
16p-n Junction: DriftElectric field in the depletion region sweeps minority carriers across the junction. Opposes diffusion.
17Depletion RegionA space-charge region on either side of the junction depleted of mobile charge carriers, containing only immobile positive and negative ions.
18Barrier Potential ()The potential difference developed across the depletion region. At equilibrium, , and net current is zero.
19Forward Biasing MechanismDepletion width decreases. Effective barrier becomes . Majority carriers easily cross, resulting in a large current (mA).
20Reverse Biasing MechanismDepletion width increases. Effective barrier becomes . Current is solely due to minority carriers (drift), very small (μA).
21V-I Characteristics (Forward Bias)Current rises exponentially only after the applied voltage exceeds the knee voltage (which equals the barrier potential ).
22V-I Characteristics (Reverse Bias)Reverse current remains almost constant () until , at which covalent bonds break, and reverse current sharply increases.
23Dynamic Resistance ()Resistance is not constant (non-ohmic device). It is the reciprocal of the slope of the V-I curve at a particular operating point.
24Half-Wave RectifierUses one diode. Conducts only during the positive half-cycle of the AC input. Output is discontinuous pulsating DC.
25Full-Wave RectifierUses two diodes and a center-tapped transformer.D1 conducts in positive half, D2 in negative half.Continuous pulsating DC output.
