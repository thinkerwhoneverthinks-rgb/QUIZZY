1 Dynamic Equilibrium Rate of forward reaction = Rate of reverse reaction At equilibrium, concentrations remain constant Closed system required; both reactions occur simultaneously; not static equilibrium
2 Equilibrium Constant (Kc) Kc = [C]c[D]d / [A]a[B]b [ ] = molar equilibrium concentration; Kc depends only on temperature; pure solids and liquids are omitted from expression
3 Equilibrium Constant (Kp) Kp = (PC)c(PD)d / (PA)a (PB)b Pressure in bar; standard state = 1 bar Used for gaseous reactions; pA, pB etc. are partial pressures at equilibrium
4 Relation: Kp & Kc gaseous reactants
5 Reverse Reaction K'c = 1/Kc K'p = 1/Kp Equilibrium constant of reverse reaction = reciprocal of forward reaction constant
6 Multiplied Equation If original equation is multiplied by n: Always specify the form of balanced chemical equation when quoting K value
7 Reaction Quotient (Qc) Qc = [C]c[D]d / [A]a[B]b (at any time t) • Qc < Kc → reaction proceeds in forward direction • Qc > Kc → reaction proceeds in reverse direction • Qc = Kc → system is at equilibrium Qc uses non-equilibrium concentrations; Kc uses only equilibrium concentrations
8 Le Chatelier’s Principle A change in any factor (concentration, pressure, temperature) shifts equilibrium to counteract that change Applies to all physical and chemical equilibria; catalyst does NOT shift equilibrium position
9 Effect of Concentration favoured Kc value does not change; only composition of equilibrium mixture changes
10 Effect of Pressure shifts toward side with fewer moles of gas with more moles of gas
11 Effect of Temperature Kc decreases (equilibrium shifts backward) Kc increases (equilibrium shifts forward) Temperature is the ONLY factor that changes the value of Kc; low T favours synthesis but slows rate → catalyst used
12 Effect of Catalyst Catalyst lowers activation energy equally for forward and reverse reactions Does NOT affect equilibrium constant or composition Increases rate of both forward and reverse reactions equally; equilibrium is reached faster

18 Lewis Concept Lewis Acid = electron pair acceptor Lewis Base = electron pair donor e.g., + : → → : Most general definition; examples: , (Lewis bases)
19 Ionization Constant of Weak Acid (Ka) HA + ⇌ + A− pKa = -log(Ka) Larger Ka = stronger acid; Ka is dimensionless; standard state concentration = 1M
20 Ionization Constant of Weak Base (K ) b MOH ⇋ M+ + pKb = -log(Kb)
21 pKa + pKb = pKw = 14 Applies to conjugate acid-base pairs; used to calculate Ka from Kb and vice versa
22 Degree of Ionization (α) α increases on dilution (Ostwald’s dilution law); decreases with common ion effect
23 Common Ion Effect Adding an ion already present in equilibrium suppresses ionization and shifts equilibrium backward Based on Le Chatelier’s principle; used in buffer preparation and selective precipitation
24 Buffer Solutions Henderson-Hasselbalch equation: pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA]) = pKa + log([Salt]/[Acid]) log([Salt]/[Base]) resists pH change on dilution or addition of small acid/base; pH unaffected by dilution
25 Salt Hydrolysis Salt of weak acid + strong base: pH > 7 Salt of strong acid + weak base: pH < 7 pH = 7 - 0.5(pKb - log C) Salt of strong acid + strong base: pH = 7 pH = 7 + 0.5(pKa + log C) pH > 7 Salt of weak acid + weak base: pH = 7 + ½(pKa − pKb) Hydrolysis = interaction of ions of salt with water; strong acid/strong base salts → no hydrolysis
26 Solubility Product (Ksp) Pure solid omitted from expression; Ksp is temperature dependent; Ksp = Qsp at equilibrium
