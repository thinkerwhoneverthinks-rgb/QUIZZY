SECTION-I — System, Surroundings & Types of Processes
1 Thermodynamic System & Surroundings System: The specific portion of matter under study. Surroundings: Everything outside the system. Universe: System + Surroundings Boundary: Real or imaginary wall separating system from surroundings. Types of Systems: Open: Exchange both matter & energy — e.g. open beaker, boiling water in open pan Closed: Exchange energy only, not matter — e.g. gas in a piston-cylinder Isolated: No exchange of either matter and energy — e.g. ideal thermos flask, bomb calorimeter
2 State of a System & State Variables A system's state is defined by its macroscopic properties: T, P, V, n, composition. State function (property): value depends only on current state, not path taken Examples of state functions: U, H, S, G, T, P, V Path functions: q (heat) and w (work) Extensive properties: depend on amount or mass-volume, U, H, S, G Intensive properties: independent of amount or mass— T, P, density, molar properties
3 Types of Thermodynamic Processes Adiabatic: q = 0 (no heat exchange with surroundings) Reversible process: Carried out infinitely slowly; system always in equilibrium; gives maximum work Irreversible process: Spontaneous;
