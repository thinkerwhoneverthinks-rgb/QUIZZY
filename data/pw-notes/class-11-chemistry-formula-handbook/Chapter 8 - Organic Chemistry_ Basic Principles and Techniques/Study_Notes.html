SECTION-VII — Purification Techniques for Organic Compounds
20 Crystallisation Principle: Compound more soluble in hot solvent than cold; impurities remain in solution on cooling Procedure: Dissolve in minimum hot solvent Fractional crystallisation: Mixture of solids separated based on different solubilities at different temperatures Examples: Purification of naphthalene, benzoic acid, aspirin Choice of solvent: 1. Should dissolve compound well when hot, poorly when cold 2. Should not react with compound 3. Impurities should be very soluble (stay in filtrate) Note: Animal charcoal (decolourising charcoal) is added to the hot solution to remove coloured impurities before filtering.
21 Distillation Simple distillation: For mixtures of liquids with large bp difference solid Fractional distillation: uses fractionating column Distillation under reduced pressure (vacuum distillation): For high-boiling compounds that decompose before boiling; bp lowered under reduced pressure Steam distillation: For compounds immiscible with water and high bp; passed steam to carry compound 1 atm Principle of steam distillation: A mixture of immiscible liquids boils when sum of vapour pressures = atmospheric pressure, i.e., at temperature below bp of either component Note: Steam distillation is possible only if: (i) compound is immiscible with water (ii) compound is volatile with steam (iii) compound is thermally stable bp of mixture < bp of water: Always true in steam distillation
22 Solvent Extraction Principle: Compound distributed between two immiscible solvents according to its relative solubility (partition coefficient) Procedure: Aqueous solution + organic Repeated extraction: More efficient than single extraction using same total volume Example: Extracting benzoic acid from water using ether Note: Multiple small-volume extractions are more efficient than one large-volume extraction. This follows from the partition law mathematics. Common organic solvents for extraction: Diethyl ether, dichloromethane , ethyl acetate, chloroform
23 Chromatography Principle: Separation based on differential movement of components between stationary and mobile phases Stationary phase: solid or immobile liquid on solid support Mobile phase: liquid or gas = distance moved by substance / distance moved by solvent Rf is characteristic of substance and solvent system Types of chromatography: TLC (Thin Layer Chromatography): silica gel on glass plate; visualised by UV or iodine Column chromatography: silica/alumina in glass column; mixture loaded, eluted with solvents of increasing polarity Paper chromatography: cellulose paper as stationary phase; water as stationary liquid; ascending or descending Note: Rf value is always between 0 and 1. Rf = 1 means compound moves with solvent front; Rf = 0 means compound did not move at all. Application: Separation of amino acid mixtures, pigments, drugs Adsorption chromatography: column / TLC — compound adsorbed on stationary phase Partition chromatography: paper / HPLC — compound partitions between two liquid phases
