INTRODUCTION TO THREE DIMENSIONAL GEOMETRY

TOPIC1

NEED FOR 3D COORDINATES

1Definition & Introduction

  • To locate a point in space uniquely, we need an ordered triplet (x, y, z).
  • In a plane, two perpendicular distances from two axes fix a point: (x, y).
  • In space, two numbers still allow movement up/down; a third number removes this ambiguity.
  • The three coordinates represent perpendicular distances from three mutually perpendicular reference planes.
  • Real-life locations (aeroplane, ball in flight, ceiling fan tip) are not confined to a plane, so 3D coordinates are needed.

2Diagram

A point P in space is fixed by three independent perpendicular distances along x, y and z.

3Key Concepts

  • 🧱Room model: two adjacent walls + floor are three mutually perpendicular planes.
  • 📏Coordinates are perpendicular distances: x from YZ-plane, y from ZX-plane, z from XY-plane.
  • 🧭Order matters: (x, y, z) is an ordered triplet; swapping changes the point.
  • 🎯With only (x, y), the point is fixed only on a vertical line above/below the plane.
  • ✈️Navigation: aircraft position needs east–west, north–south, and altitude (three independent measures).

4WHY 2 NUMBERS FAIL IN SPACE

  • In the plane, (x, y) pins a point by distances from two perpendicular axes.
  • In space, after fixing (x, y), the point can still slide along a line parallel to z-axis.
  • So infinitely many points share the same (x, y); a third number z selects exactly one.
  • Geometric idea: 2D needs two independent directions; 3D needs three independent directions.

5REAL-LIFE INTERPRETATION (ROOM / MOTION)

  • Room example: locate a bulb tip by distances from two walls and height from floor.
  • Ball thrown in space: at any instant, its position needs three measures: left-right, front-back, up-down.
  • Aeroplane: needs two horizontal coordinates plus altitude; 2D map alone is incomplete.
  • Thus 3D coordinates model actual space, not just drawings on paper.

7Solved Q&A

1
A point in a room is 3 m from the left wall and 4 m from the back wall. Can you locate it uniquely? What extra information is needed?
Answer:
Distances from two perpendicular walls fix the point only on a vertical line above the floor point.
Any height on that vertical line has the same two wall distances.
So we also need the height from the floor (third distance) to pinpoint it.
2
Explain why (x, y) is sufficient on a map, but (x, y, z) is needed to specify an aeroplane’s position.
Answer:
A map represents a plane (Earth’s surface projection), so positions are on a 2D sheet.
An aeroplane can be above the same map point at many altitudes.
Altitude is independent of horizontal location, so it must be given separately as z.
✓  Map points lie in 2D, but an aircraft adds altitude; hence three coordinates are required.

8⭐Important Points

  • Space needs three independent directions; hence three numbers.
  • x, y, z are perpendicular distances from YZ-, ZX-, and XY-planes respectively.
  • Given (x, y), the point is still not fixed; it lies on a line parallel to the z-axis.
  • The coordinates are always written as an ordered triplet (x, y, z).
  • Room model (two walls + floor) is the standard intuition used in exams.

9❌Common Mistakes

  • Thinking (x, y) determines a unique point even in space.
  • Mixing meanings: writing x as distance from x-axis (it is from the YZ-plane in 3D).
  • Treating (x, y, z) as unordered; swapping entries gives a different point.
  • Assuming the floor is always z = 0 without stating the chosen coordinate system.

TOPIC2

3D AXES AND PLANES

1Definition & Introduction

  • A 3D rectangular coordinate system is formed by three mutually perpendicular axes OX, OY, OZ meeting at the origin O.
  • These axes arise as lines of intersection of three mutually perpendicular planes through O.
  • The planes determined by each pair of axes are the coordinate planes: XY-plane (XOY), YZ-plane (YOZ), ZX-plane (ZOX).
  • Convention: take XY-plane as the plane of the paper; z-axis is perpendicular to it (+z along OZ, −z along OZ′).
  • The three coordinate planes divide space into 8 regions called octants.

2Diagram

Coordinate planes meet at O; their intersections are the x-, y-, z-axes.

3Key Concepts

  • 🧭Axes are mutually perpendicular: OX ⟂ OY, OY ⟂ OZ, OZ ⟂ OX.
  • 🧱Each coordinate plane contains two axes and is ⟂ to the remaining axis.
  • ➕Signs depend on chosen directions of +x, +y, +z (standard NCERT setup).
  • 🧩On x-axis: (x,0,0); on XY-plane: (x,y,0); on YZ-plane: (0,y,z); on ZX-plane: (x,0,z).

4ORIENTATION & SIGN CONVENTION (NCERT SETUP)

  • Take XOY (XY-plane) as the plane of the paper.
  • Z′OZ is perpendicular to the XY-plane.
  • Along OZ is +z; along OZ′ is −z.
  • In front of YZ-plane along OX is +x; behind along OX′ is −x.
  • To one chosen side of ZX-plane along OY is +y; opposite along OY′ is −y.

5COORDINATE PLANES & OCTANTS

  • XY-plane: points satisfy z = 0; contains x- and y-axes.
  • YZ-plane: points satisfy x = 0; contains y- and z-axes.
  • ZX-plane: points satisfy y = 0; contains z- and x-axes.
  • The three planes split space into 8 octants.
  • Octant depends on the signs of (x, y, z).

7Solved Q&A

1
A point P lies on the YZ-plane and has y = −4 and z = 6. Write its coordinates.
Answer:
On the YZ-plane, the x-coordinate is 0.
So the coordinates are (0, y, z) = (0, −4, 6).
2
A point is (5, 0, −2). Identify (i) the plane/axis it lies on, and (ii) its perpendicular distances from the three coordinate planes (in coordinate units).
Answer:
Since y = 0, the point lies on the ZX-plane (ZOX-plane).
Perpendicular distance from YZ-plane depends on |x|, so it is |5| = 5.
Perpendicular distance from ZX-plane depends on |y|, so it is |0| = 0.
Perpendicular distance from XY-plane depends on |z|, so it is |−2| = 2.
✓  (i) Lies on ZX-plane. (ii) Distances: from YZ = 5, from ZX = 0, from XY = 2.

8⭐Important Points

  • Three mutually perpendicular planes through O generate the 3D rectangular coordinate system.
  • Coordinate planes are named by the axes in them: XY, YZ, ZX.
  • Each coordinate plane is perpendicular to the remaining axis (e.g. XY-plane ⟂ z-axis).
  • Origin is the common intersection point: O(0,0,0).
  • Plane tests: z=0 (XY), x=0 (YZ), y=0 (ZX).

9❌Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up plane names: writing ZX-plane as x=0 (wrong; x=0 is YZ-plane).
  • On an axis, two coordinates are zero (not one).
  • Confusing signed coordinates with distances; distances are non-negative |x|, |y|, |z|.
  • Assuming +y is always left/right; it depends on the chosen convention.

10💡Memory Technique

TOPIC3

SIGN CONVENTION IN SPACE (3D)

1Definition & Introduction

  • Sign convention assigns positive/negative directions along x, y, z using the three coordinate planes XY, YZ, ZX.
  • Distances measured upwards from XY-plane along OZ are +z; downwards along OZ′ are −z.
  • Distances measured in front of YZ-plane along OX are +x; back of YZ-plane along OX′ are −x.
  • Distances measured to the right of ZX-plane along OY are +y; to the left of ZX-plane along OY′ are −y.
  • For a point P(x,y,z): signs of (x,y,z) depend on which side of each coordinate plane P lies.

2Diagram

Positive/negative directions are fixed by which side of XY, YZ, ZX planes the point lies.

3Key Concepts

  • 📌Each coordinate is a signed perpendicular distance from a coordinate plane: x from YZ, y from ZX, z from XY.
  • ⬆️OZ is the positive z-direction; OZ′ is the negative z-direction.
  • ➡️OX is positive in front of YZ-plane; OX′ is negative behind it.
  • ↔️OY is positive to the right of ZX-plane; OY′ is negative to the left.
  • 🧩Three coordinate planes split space into 8 octants; the sign triple (x,y,z) identifies the octant.

4HOW TO DECIDE SIGN OF EACH COORDINATE

  • x: check position w.r.t. YZ-plane (x=0 plane). Front along OX ⇒ +, back along OX′ ⇒ −.
  • y: check position w.r.t. ZX-plane (y=0 plane). Right along OY ⇒ +, left along OY′ ⇒ −.
  • z: check position w.r.t. XY-plane (z=0 plane). Upwards along OZ ⇒ +, downwards along OZ′ ⇒ −.
  • If P lies on a coordinate plane, the coordinate perpendicular to it is 0.

5OCTANTS: SIGN TRIPLES (EXAM QUICK MAP)

  • I: (+,+,+), II: (−,+,+), III: (−,−,+), IV: (+,−,+).
  • V: (+,+,−), VI: (−,+,−), VII: (−,−,−), VIII: (+,−,−).
  • Opposite octants have all signs reversed (e.g. I ↔ VII).

1
A point P is 4 units behind the YZ-plane, 3 units to the right of the ZX-plane, and 2 units above the XY-plane. Write P(x,y,z) and name the octant.
Answer:
Behind YZ-plane ⇒ along OX′ ⇒ x is negative ⇒ x=−4.
Right of ZX-plane ⇒ along OY ⇒ y is positive ⇒ y=+3.
Above XY-plane ⇒ along OZ ⇒ z is positive ⇒ z=+2.
Sign triple is (−,+,+) ⇒ octant II.
✓  P(−4, 3, 2), in octant II.
2
Without calculation, decide where the point Q(0, −5, 7) lies: axis/plane/octant? Justify using sign convention.
Answer:
x=0 ⇒ Q lies on the YZ-plane (because YZ-plane is x=0).
y=−5 ⇒ Q is to the left of the ZX-plane (along OY′).
z=7 ⇒ Q is above the XY-plane (along OZ).
Since one coordinate is 0, Q is not in any octant (octants exclude the coordinate planes).
✓  Q lies on the YZ-plane, left of ZX-plane, above XY-plane; not in any octant.

8⭐Important Points

  • Positive/negative directions are defined relative to coordinate planes, not relative to the viewer.
  • x, y, z are signed distances from YZ, ZX, XY planes respectively.
  • Up/down convention: OZ is +z, OZ′ is −z (vertical if XY is taken as horizontal).
  • On axes: x-axis points are (x,0,0), y-axis (0,y,0), z-axis (0,0,z).
  • To identify octant quickly, read signs of (x,y,z) in order.

9❌Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up planes: writing x=0 for ZX-plane (correct is y=0).
  • Taking +/− from drawing orientation; ignoring fixed directions OX, OY, OZ.
  • Assuming points with a zero coordinate belong to an octant.
  • Swapping meanings: ‘right of ZX-plane’ incorrectly linked to x instead of y.

10💡Memory Technique

“Plane tells the coordinate that becomes zero”
YZ ⇒ x=0, ZX ⇒ y=0, XY ⇒ z=0.Remember: the plane name shows the axes it contains; the missing axis is the perpendicular coordinate.

TOPIC4

OCTANTS AND THEIR SIGNS

1Definition & Introduction

  • Octants are the eight regions of 3D space formed by the coordinate planes XY, YZ, ZX.
  • The planes XY (z=0), YZ (x=0), ZX (y=0) intersect at origin O and are mutually perpendicular.
  • Each octant has a fixed sign pattern for coordinates (x, y, z).
  • Octants are named using axis directions: X or X′, Y or Y′, Z or Z′ (e.g., XOYZ, X′OY′Z′).
  • If any coordinate is 0, the point lies on a coordinate plane/axis and not strictly inside an octant.

2Diagram

Three coordinate planes meet at O and divide space into 8 octants; octant I has (+,+,+).

3Key Concepts

  • 📌Octant I (XOYZ) is where x, y, z are all positive.
  • 🧩Changing the sign of one coordinate reflects the point across the corresponding coordinate plane.
  • 🧠Use the sign triple (+/−, +/−, +/−) to map directly to an octant using the octant sign table.
  • 📐Coordinate planes: XY separates +z and −z; YZ separates +x and −x; ZX separates +y and −y.

4TABLE: OCTANTS (I TO VIII) AND SIGN PATTERNS

  • I: XOYZ ⇒ (x,y,z) = (+,+,+)
  • II: X′OYZ ⇒ (−,+,+)
  • III: X′OY′Z ⇒ (−,−,+)
  • IV: XOY′Z ⇒ (+,−,+)
  • V: XOYZ′ ⇒ (+,+,−)

8⭐Important Points

  • Coordinate planes split space into 8 octants; each octant fixes signs of (x,y,z).
  • YZ-plane (x=0) separates +x and −x half-spaces.
  • ZX-plane (y=0) separates +y and −y half-spaces.
  • XY-plane (z=0) separates +z and −z half-spaces.
  • If any coordinate is 0, the point is on a coordinate plane (or axis), not inside an octant.

  • Treating a point with a zero coordinate as lying in an octant (it lies on a plane/axis).
  • Confusing the octant name order (X/X′, then Y/Y′, then Z/Z′) while writing XOYZ-type names.

TOPIC5

COORDINATES OF A POINT (3D)

1Definition & Introduction

  • Coordinates of P are the ordered triplet P(x, y, z) in a fixed 3D rectangular system.
  • Drop perpendicular PM to XY-plane; M is the foot (projection of P on XY-plane).
  • From M, draw perpendicular ML to x-axis, meeting it at L (so ML ∥ y-axis).
  • Define OL = x, LM = y, MP = z (with signs as per chosen positive directions).
  • Thus, to each point in space corresponds exactly one ordered triplet of real numbers, and conversely.

2Diagram

Coordinates via projections: PM ⟂ XY-plane at M; ML ⟂ x-axis at L; OL=x, LM=y, MP=z.

3Key Concepts

  • 🧭Projection idea: M fixes (x, y) in XY-plane; then z lifts the point to P.
  • 📐x, y, z are signed perpendicular distances from YZ, ZX, XY planes respectively.
  • 🔁One-to-one correspondence: Point P ↔ ordered triplet (x, y, z).
  • ➕Quick location checks: XY-plane → z=0; YZ-plane → x=0; ZX-plane → y=0.

4HOW TO ASSIGN (x, y, z) TO A GIVEN POINT P

  • Fix rectangular axes OX, OY, OZ and coordinate planes XY, YZ, ZX.
  • Drop PM ⟂ XY-plane; M is the projection of P on XY-plane.
  • In XY-plane, draw ML ⟂ x-axis meeting it at L (so ML ∥ y-axis).
  • Read signed lengths: OL = x, LM = y, MP = z; write P(x, y, z).

5CONVERSE (ORDERED TRIPLET → POINT) + WHY ONE-TO-ONE

  • Given (x, y, z): mark L on x-axis with OL = x (use sign).
  • From L, move parallel to y-axis to reach M in XY-plane with LM = y.
  • From M, go perpendicular to XY-plane to reach P with MP = z.
  • Uniqueness: fixed perpendicular constructions give exactly one P for each (x, y, z), and vice versa.

8⭐Important Points

  • Coordinates are written as an ordered triplet: (x, y, z); order cannot be changed.
  • In projection method: P → M by dropping perpendicular to XY-plane; then M → L perpendicular to x-axis in XY-plane.
  • Signs of x, y, z depend on the chosen positive directions of axes/planes (different octants give different signs).
  • Each triplet (x, y, z) locates exactly one point in space (existence + uniqueness).
  • Quick checks: XY-plane ⇒ z=0; YZ-plane ⇒ x=0; ZX-plane ⇒ y=0.

9❌Common Mistakes

  • Mixing plane–coordinate link: x↔YZ, y↔ZX, z↔XY (distance from plane that misses the letter).
  • Forgetting signs when the point lies in other octants.
  • Swapping y and z while reading the projection idea (M is in XY-plane, so it fixes x and y).
  • Writing coordinates in a different order like (y, x, z); ordered triplet means position matters.

TOPIC6

PARALLEL PLANES METHOD (3D COORDINATES)

1Definition & Introduction

  • Parallel planes method: Through point P, draw three planes parallel to the coordinate planes YZ, ZX, XY; their common intersection point is P.
  • The plane parallel to YZ has equation x=constant and meets the x-axis at A=(x,0,0). Similarly, the plane parallel to ZX meets the y-axis at B=(0,y,0) and the plane parallel to XY meets the z-axis at C=(0,0,z).
  • Define OA=x, OB=y, OC=z; then P has coordinates P(x,y,z).
  • x, y, z are signed perpendicular distances of P from planes YZ, ZX, XY, with signs decided by the octant.
  • Conversely, given (x,y,z), draw planes through (x,0,0), (0,y,0), (0,0,z) parallel to YZ, ZX, XY; their intersection gives P.

2Diagram

Through P, planes ∥ YZ, ∥ ZX, ∥ XY correspond to x=const, y=const, z=const; they meet axes at A=(x,0,0), B=(0,y,0), C=(0,0,z).

3Key Concepts

  • KCPlane ∥ YZ has equation x=constant; it fixes the x-coordinate.
  • KCPlane ∥ ZX has equation y=constant; it fixes the y-coordinate.
  • KCPlane ∥ XY has equation z=constant; it fixes the z-coordinate.
  • KCx=signed distance from YZ-plane; y from ZX-plane; z from XY-plane.
  • KCThree mutually perpendicular planes (x=const, y=const, z=const) intersect at exactly one point P.

4WORKING (CONSTRUCTION & INTERPRETATION)

  • Through P, draw plane ∥ YZ. It has form x=constant and meets x-axis at A=(x,0,0).
  • Through P, draw plane ∥ ZX. It has form y=constant and meets y-axis at B=(0,y,0).
  • Through P, draw plane ∥ XY. It has form z=constant and meets z-axis at C=(0,0,z).
  • The three planes x=xP, y=yP, z=zP are mutually perpendicular and intersect at a unique point P(x,y,z).
  • Meaning: x, y, z are signed perpendicular distances from YZ, ZX, XY planes respectively.

5FAST CHECKS (SPECIAL LOCATIONS)

  • On x-axis: (x,0,0) because it lies on both ZX (y=0) and XY (z=0) planes.
  • On y-axis: (0,y,0); on z-axis: (0,0,z).
  • In XY-plane: (x,y,0); in YZ-plane: (0,y,z); in ZX-plane: (x,0,z).
  • Sign check: if point is behind YZ-plane then x<0; left of ZX-plane then y<0; below XY-plane then z<0.

SPECIAL POINT LOCATIONS (3D COORDINATES)

1Definition & Introduction

  • A point in 3D is written as an ordered triplet (x, y, z) in a rectangular coordinate system.
  • If a point lies on a coordinate axis, the other two coordinates are 0 (the point lies on two coordinate planes).
  • If a point lies on a coordinate plane, the coordinate perpendicular to that plane is 0.
  • Origin is the common intersection of all three axes and all three coordinate planes: (0,0,0).
  • Coordinate-plane constraints: YZ ⇒ x=0, ZX ⇒ y=0, XY ⇒ z=0.

2Diagram

Coordinate planes impose one zero-coordinate; axes impose two zero-coordinates.

3Key Concepts

  • 🧭Axis rule: on x-axis ⇒ y=0 and z=0; similarly for y- and z-axes.
  • 📄Plane rule: in XY-plane ⇒ z=0; in YZ-plane ⇒ x=0; in ZX-plane ⇒ y=0.
  • 🔁Reading trick: the missing letter’s coordinate is zero (e.g., YZ-plane misses X ⇒ x=0).
  • ✅To test a location: check which coordinate(s) equal 0; zeros decide axis/plane.

4ZERO-CONSTRAINT RULE (MOST USED IN QUESTIONS)

  • On x-axis: (x,0,0) because point is in both XY (z=0) and ZX (y=0).
  • On y-axis: (0,y,0) because point is in both XY (z=0) and YZ (x=0).
  • On z-axis: (0,0,z) because point is in both YZ (x=0) and ZX (y=0).
  • In planes: YZ → (0,y,z), ZX → (x,0,z), XY → (x,y,0).

5HOW TO IDENTIFY LOCATION FROM COORDINATES

  • Exactly two coordinates are zero ⇒ point lies on an axis (named by the nonzero coordinate).
  • Exactly one coordinate is zero ⇒ point lies in a coordinate plane (named by the two nonzero coordinate letters).
  • No coordinate is zero ⇒ point is in an octant (not on any axis/plane).
  • All three are zero ⇒ origin.

DISTANCE FORMULA IN 3D

1Definition & Introduction

  • Distance between P(x1,y1,z1) and Q(x2,y2,z2) is the length of segment PQ in 3D.
  • Construct a rectangular parallelepiped with diagonal PQ; its edges are |x2−x1|, |y2−y1|, |z2−z1|.
  • Apply Pythagoras twice using two right triangles formed on adjacent faces.
  • Squared distance adds squared coordinate differences: PQ2 = Δx2+Δy2+Δz2.
  • Special case: distance from origin O(0,0,0) to Q(x,y,z) is OQ = √(x2+y2+z2).

2Diagram

Rectangular parallelepiped with space diagonal PQ; edges represent Δx, Δy, Δz.

3Key Concepts

  • 📦Think of Δx, Δy, Δz as three perpendicular edges of a box.
  • 🧩Use two right triangles: first on a face, then with the space diagonal.
  • 🧮Always compute PQ2 first; take square root at the end.
  • 🎯From origin: just plug x1=y1=z1=0.

4PYTHAGORAS-BASED DERIVATION (BOX METHOD)

  • Let P(x1,y1,z1), Q(x2,y2,z2); define Δx=x2−x1, Δy=y2−y1, Δz=z2−z1.
  • On a face, diagonal squared = Δx2+Δy2 (2D distance on that face).
  • Space diagonal squared = (face diagonal)2 + Δz2 ⇒ Δx2+Δy2+Δz2.

5FAST APPLICATION TIPS (EXAM SPEED)

  • Use differences first; signs don’t matter after squaring, but differences must be correct.
  • If one coordinate is same, its term becomes 0 (reduces to 2D or 1D distance).
  • To avoid √ early, compare squared lengths for right angle/collinearity checks.

6Formulas

On a face rectangle (right triangle in a coordinate plane):
AQ2 = (Δx)2 + (Δz)2
Then use the right triangle with PQ as hypotenuse:
PQ2 = (Δy)2 + AQ2
Substitute and simplify:
PQ2 = (Δx)2 + (Δy)2 + (Δz)2
Take square root (distance is non-negative):
PQ = √((Δx)2+(Δy)2+(Δz)2)
P(x1,y1,z1) =First point in 3D rectangular coordinate system
Q(x2,y2,z2) =Second point
Δx, Δy, Δz =Coordinate differences: x2−x1, y2−y1, z2−z1
O(0,0,0) =Origin
Most important result
PQ = √((x2−x1)2+(y2−y1)2+(z2−z1)2)

7Solved Examples

1
Find the distance between P(1, −3, 4) and Q(−4, 1, 2).
Solution:
Compute differences: Δx=−4−1=−5, Δy=1−(−3)=4, Δz=2−4=−2.
Square and add: PQ2=(−5)2+42+(−2)2=25+16+4=45.
Distance: PQ=√45=√(9×5)=3√5.
✓  PQ = 3√5 units
2
Find the distance of Q(−2, 6, −3) from the origin O(0,0,0).
Solution:
Use origin formula: OQ=√(x2+y2+z2).
Substitute: OQ=√((−2)2+62+(−3)2)=√(4+36+9)=√49.
So OQ=7.
✓  OQ = 7 units

8⭐Important Points

  • Distance is always non-negative; final step is principal square root.
  • Order of points doesn’t matter: swapping P and Q changes signs of Δ’s only.
  • If two coordinates match, distance reduces to 2D formula in the remaining plane.
  • Use squared distances to check relations quickly (e.g., compare a2+b2 with c2).
  • Origin distance is a direct special case of the same formula.

9❌Common Mistakes

  • Using (x2+x1) instead of (x2−x1).
  • Forgetting to square a difference or squaring only one term.
  • Taking √ too early and then adding roots incorrectly.
  • Arithmetic sign errors in Δy when subtracting a negative value.

APPLICATIONS OF DISTANCE (3D)

1Definition & Introduction

  • 3D distance formula converts coordinate conditions into length relations between points in space.
  • Collinearity can be tested using segment-addition: PQ + QR = PR (with Q between P and R).
  • Triangle type checks use squared lengths: compare a2 + b2 with c2 (take c as the largest side).
  • Quadrilateral properties can be verified by equal opposite sides or diagonal properties using distances.
  • Locus equations arise from distance constraints like PA = PB or PA2 + PB2 = constant (a real locus exists only if the constant is at least the minimum possible value).

2Diagram

Compute lengths using 3D distance, then apply standard length relations to conclude geometry.

3Key Concepts

  • 🧭Collinear test: compute PQ, QR, PR and check PQ + QR = PR.
  • 📐Right triangle: if largest side is c, check a2 + b2 = c2.
  • ▱Parallelogram: show AB = CD and BC = AD (or diagonals bisect).
  • ▭Rectangle: show parallelogram first, then check diagonals: AC = BD.
  • 🎯Locus PA = PB: the perpendicular bisector plane from PA2 = PB2 (linear equation).
  • ⚖️Locus PA2 + PB2 = const gives a sphere; it becomes a single point at the minimum, and no real points if const is smaller than the minimum.

4CHECKLIST: WHAT TO COMPUTE FIRST

  • Prefer squared distances to avoid √ until the final comparison.
  • For collinearity, identify the possible middle point before using PQ + QR = PR.
  • For right triangle, compare the largest squared side with sum of other two.
  • For parallelogram, verify both pairs of opposite sides equal using distances.
  • For rectangle, show parallelogram first, then test diagonals: AC = BD.

5LOCI FROM DISTANCE CONDITIONS (FAST EXPANSION TRICK)

  • Equidistant from A and B: set PA2 = PB2 to cancel x2, y2, z2 → linear plane (perpendicular bisector plane).
  • Sum of squares fixed: expand PA2 + PB2 = const → sphere after completing squares (or no real points if const is too small).
  • If M is midpoint of AB, then PA2 + PB2 = 2PM2 + AB22, so the minimum is AB22 attained at P = M.

7Solved Examples

1
Using distance formula, show that P(−2, 3, 5), Q(1, 2, 3), R(7, 0, −1) are collinear.
Solution:
Compute PQ2 = (1+2)2 + (2−3)2 + (3−5)2 = 9 + 1 + 4 = 14 ⇒ PQ = √14.
Compute QR2 = (7−1)2 + (0−2)2 + (−1−3)2 = 36 + 4 + 16 = 56 ⇒ QR = √56 = 2√14.
Compute PR2 = (7+2)2 + (0−3)2 + (−1−5)2 = 81 + 9 + 36 = 126 ⇒ PR = √126 = 3√14.
Check segment addition: PQ + QR = √14 + 2√14 = 3√14 = PR.
✓  Yes. Since PQ + QR = PR, the three points are collinear (Q lies between P and R).
2
Find the locus equation of points P(x,y,z) such that PA2 + PB2 = 2k2, where A(3,4,5), B(−1,3,−7).
Solution:
PA2 = (x−3)2 + (y−4)2 + (z−5)2.
PB2 = (x+1)2 + (y−3)2 + (z+7)2.
Add and expand: (x−3)2+(x+1)2 = 2x2 − 4x + 10.
(y−4)2+(y−3)2 = 2y2 − 14y + 25.
(z−5)2+(z+7)2 = 2z2 + 4z + 74.
So PA2+PB2 = 2x2+2y2+2z2 − 4x − 14y + 4z + (10+25+74) = 2x2+2y2+2z2 − 4x − 14y + 4z + 109.
✓  Locus: 2x2 + 2y2 + 2z2 − 4x − 14y + 4z = 2k2 − 109.

8⭐Important Points

  • Use squared distances for comparisons: avoids radicals and reduces arithmetic errors.
  • In collinearity by distances, the equality must match the correct order of points on the line.
  • Right-angle test needs largest side as hypotenuse; otherwise comparison is meaningless.
  • To prove rectangle: parallelogram alone is insufficient; also need diagonals AC = BD.
  • Locus PA = PB becomes a plane (linear equation) after expansion.

9❌Common Mistakes

  • Using PQ + PR = QR without checking which point lies between the other two.
  • Comparing AB + BC with AC using squared lengths (should use actual lengths there).
  • For right triangle, not identifying the largest side before applying Pythagoras.
  • Declaring rectangle from AB = CD, BC = AD only (that proves parallelogram).
