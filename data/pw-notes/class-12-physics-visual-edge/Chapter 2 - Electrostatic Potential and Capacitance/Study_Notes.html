ELECTROSTATIC POTENTIAL AND CAPACITANCE

TOPIC1
Stored work against a conservative force field; energy converts between U and K

POTENTIAL ENERGY BASICS

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Potential energy is the work stored when an external agent moves a body against a force field (in quasi-static motion).
  • If the constraint is removed and only conservative forces act, U decreases and the same amount of K increases (so K+U stays constant).
  • Conservative force: work between two points depends only on initial & final positions, not the path.
  • For conservative forces, a unique potential energy function U(position) can be defined (up to a constant).
  • Only potential energy differences are physical; U can be shifted by any constant reference.

🖼️Diagram

External work against a conservative field is stored as U; on release it converts to K (if only conservative forces act).

🎯Key Concepts

  • 🧭Path independence: WA→B same for all paths ⇒ potential energy is definable.
  • 🔁Energy link (conservative): Wfield=−ΔU and Δ(K+U)=0.
  • 🧲Coulomb force is conservative (central, inverse-square): enables electrostatic potential energy.
  • 🌍Gravity (near Earth): U=mgh; gravity is conservative (ignoring air drag).
  • 🪀Spring force: F=−kx (conservative) ⇒ U=12kx2.
  • 📌Inverse-square forces: |F|∝1/r2 ⇒ with U(∞)=0, potential energy varies as 1/r.

🔎WORK ↔ POTENTIAL ENERGY (SIGNS)

  • Quasi-static transfer: Fext=−Ffield ⇒ net force zero, so ΔK=0 during transfer.
  • Work by external agent: Wext=∫Fext·dl = −∫Ffield·dl.
  • Potential energy change: ΔU=UB−UA=Wext (definition for conservative field).
  • Work by field: Wfield=−ΔU. Along the field direction, Wfield>0 ⇒ U decreases.
  • Like charges closer: field opposes motion ⇒ Wext>0 ⇒ U increases.

⚙️CONSERVATIVE FORCES: THREE HIGH-YIELD EXAMPLES

  • Gravity: central attractive; near Earth ΔU=mgΔh; depends only on heights, not path.
  • Spring: restoring force proportional to displacement; ΔU depends only on initial/final x.
  • Coulomb: central inverse-square |F|=k|Qqr2| ⇒ definable electrostatic U(r) with reference U(∞)=0.
  • Inverse-square takeaway: F falls as 1/r2, but for inverse-square forces (with U(∞)=0) potential energy varies as 1/r.
  • Non-conservative contrast: friction makes work path-dependent; mechanical energy is not conserved.

TOPIC2
Quasi-static external work stored as potential energy: ΔU=Wext, path-independent; only differences matter

ELECTROSTATIC POTENTIAL ENERGY (WORK DEFINITION)

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Electrostatic potential energy change: ΔU=UP−UR=WRP done by external agent in quasi-static transfer.
  • Use a test charge q small enough to not disturb source charges; source configuration held fixed.
  • Quasi-static means v is infinitesimal and ΔK=0; external work goes entirely into ΔU.
  • At each point: Fext=−FE, so net force is zero during slow motion.
  • Work by field is opposite: Wfield=−Wext=−ΔU for quasi-static transfer.

🖼️Diagram

Quasi-static transport: external force balances electric force; external work becomes ΔU.

🎯Key Concepts

  • 🧪Test charge concept: take q → small so field source is unchanged.
  • 🧘Quasi-static: no acceleration ⇒ no kinetic-energy change; energy bookkeeping becomes clean.
  • 🧭Conservative field: electrostatic work depends only on endpoints ⇒ potential energy is well-defined.
  • 🧾Additive constant freedom: U can be shifted by α; only ΔU has physical meaning.
  • ↔️Sign logic: moving against FE ⇒ Wext positive and Wfield negative.

🔎WORK DEFINITION (Eqs. 2.1, 2.2) + SIGN

  • Eq. 2.1: WRP=∫RPFext·dℓ=−∫RPFE·dℓ.
  • Eq. 2.2: ΔU=UP−UR=WRP (external work stored as potential energy).
  • If displacement is opposite to FE, then Wfield<0 and Wext>0 (so ΔU>0).
  • If displacement is along FE, then Wfield>0 and Wext<0 (so ΔU<0).

⚙️TWO CONSEQUENCES YOU MUST STATE IN ANSWERS

  • Path independence: electrostatic work between two points depends only on endpoints (conservative field).
  • Additive constant freedom: replacing U by (U+α) leaves ΔU unchanged.
  • Convenient reference: set U(∞)=0 ⇒ U(P)=W∞P for quasi-static bringing from infinity.

⭐Important Points

  • Electrostatic potential energy is a state function: depends only on configuration (initial and final positions).

  • Only ΔU is measurable; choosing U(∞)=0 is a convenient reference, not a law of nature.
  • Sign checkpoint: Wfield=−ΔU; if field does positive work, potential energy must decrease.

ZERO AT INFINITY (ELECTROSTATIC POTENTIAL ENERGY)

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Set the reference by defining U(∞)=0; then UP=W∞P (Eq. 2.3).
  • Here W∞P is the work done by an external agent in bringing test charge q from ∞ to point P quasi-statically.
  • Quasi-static means no acceleration: Fext=−FE at every point, so no change in kinetic energy.
  • Only differences in potential energy are physical; choosing the zero is adding a constant everywhere.
  • For like charges, bringing them closer needs positive external work, stored as electrostatic potential energy (spring compression analogy).
  • Work done by the electric field and external agent are opposite in sign: Wfield=−Wext=−ΔU (slow transport).

🖼️Diagram

Choose U(∞)=0; then the work done by external agent from ∞ to P defines UP.

🎯Key Concepts

  • KC1Operational meaning: U at a point = work required to bring q from ∞ to that point.
  • KC2Reference at infinity is convenient because interaction energy → 0 as separation → ∞ for localized charges.
  • KC3Electrostatic force is conservative ⇒ work (and ΔU) is path independent.
  • KC4Sign logic: like charges closer ⇒ U increases; unlike charges closer ⇒ U decreases.
  • KC5Spring analogy: external work compressing spring ↔ external work bringing like charges closer.

🔎WHY WE CAN SET ZERO AT INFINITY

  • Electrostatic work is conservative ⇒ ΔU depends only on endpoints, not path.
  • Adding constant α to U everywhere changes nothing measurable: (UP+α)−(UR+α)=UP−UR.
  • For localized charge configurations, electrostatic interaction energy tends to 0 as separation → ∞, so ∞ is a convenient zero.

⚙️SIGN + SPRING ANALOGY (EXAM FAVORITE)

  • Like charges: to decrease separation, you push against repulsion ⇒ Wext>0 ⇒ ΔU>0 (energy stored).
  • Unlike charges: field pulls them together ⇒ external agent does negative work in slow assembly ⇒ ΔU<0.
  • Release constraint: potential energy converts to kinetic energy as system moves to lower U.

ELECTROSTATIC POTENTIAL (V)

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Electrostatic potential at a point: work done by an external agent in bringing unit + test charge from ∞ to that point, quasi-statically.
  • Potential difference: VP − VR equals work per unit charge to move + test charge from R to P (quasi-statically).
  • Because electrostatic force is conservative in electrostatics, potential difference is path-independent (depends only on endpoints).
  • Reference choice: only differences matter; commonly set V(∞)=0 to define absolute V relative to infinity.
  • To keep V a field property, test charge must be very small so it does not disturb source charges.

🖼️Diagram

Potential at P is defined via external work per unit + test charge, with reference V(∞)=0.

🎯Key Concepts

  • 🧲V is a scalar field; superposition is algebraic (signs included).
  • 🧪Definition uses external work; for slow transport Wfield = −Wext.
  • 📌For an infinitesimal test charge: V = dWext/dq (avoids finite-q disturbance).
  • 🧭Sign sense: if field assists motion, Wext can be negative ⇒ V can be negative (with V(∞)=0).

🔎SIGNS + WHO DOES THE WORK (EXAM TRAP ZONE)

  • Quasi-static: Fext = −Fe at every point; ΔK = 0.
  • Wext is stored as ΔU; hence ΔU = Wext and Wfield = −ΔU.
  • For a single isolated point charge +Q, force on +q is repulsive; attraction requires opposite signs (or a multi-charge configuration giving net attraction).
  • Negative potential (with V(∞)=0) means: bringing unit + charge from ∞ releases energy (field does net positive work).

⚙️HOW TO STATE POTENTIAL DIFFERENCE CLEANLY

  • Eq. 2.4: VP − VR = (UP − UR)/q (q is the test charge).
  • Equivalent: VP − VR = Wext(R→P)/q for quasi-static motion.
  • If R=∞ and V(∞)=0, then V(P) = Wext(∞→P)/q.
  • Units: 1 V = 1 J C−1.

POTENTIAL OF A POINT CHARGE

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Electrostatic potential at distance r from a point charge Q: work done by an external agent per unit + test charge to bring it from ∞ to r (quasi-statically).
  • Reference choice: V(∞)=0 (allowed because only potential differences are physical).
  • For a point charge, potential depends only on distance: V=V(r) (spherical symmetry).
  • Sign: Q>0 ⇒ V>0; Q<0 ⇒ V<0 (for reference at ∞).
  • Distance dependence: V ∝ 1/r falls slower than E ∝ 1/r2.
  • Unit: volt (V) = J C−1; k=14πε0 ≈ 9×109 N m2 C−2.

🖼️Diagram

Point charge Q at origin; bring unit + test charge from ∞ to distance r along radial line.

🎯Key Concepts

  • 🧭Choose a radial path; electrostatic work is path independent (conservative field).
  • 🧮For a + unit test charge: field is E=kQr2 (r-direction with sign of Q); force on unit charge equals E.
  • ➖Radial sign convention: take dr as algebraic (inward ⇒ dr<0). Then dWext=−Fr dr gives correct sign automatically.
  • 📉Comparison idea: doubling r halves V, but makes E one-fourth.
  • 🔁Relation: radial component Er = −dV/dr. For V=kQ/r ⇒ Er=kQ/r2 (sign included).

🔎DERIVATION: V(r) FROM WORK DONE (Eqs. 2.5–2.8)

  • At distance r' , force on a + unit test charge: Fr(r')=kQr'2 (signed radial component; outward for Q>0, inward for Q<0).
  • Quasi-static: Fext=−F so dWext=Fext·dl=−F·dl.
  • Choose radial path so dl is along radius and equals dr' (algebraic): dWext=−Fr(r') dr'=−kQr'2dr'.
  • Total work from ∞ to r: W=∫∞r(−kQr'2dr')=kQr.
  • By definition with unit + charge: V(r)=W=kQr, valid for any sign of Q (with V(∞)=0).

⚙️SIGN + DISTANCE DEPENDENCE (HIGH-YIELD CONCEPTS)

  • Q>0: bringing + unit charge inward is against repulsion ⇒ external work > 0 ⇒ V>0.
  • Q<0: attraction aids inward motion ⇒ external work < 0 ⇒ V<0 (with V(∞)=0).
  • At fixed Q: V ∝ 1/r but E ∝ 1/r2; field weakens faster than potential.
  • Ratio trick: V2V1=r1r2 and E2E1=(r1r2)2 (for magnitudes).

POTENTIAL OF ELECTRIC DIPOLE

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Potential at a point due to a dipole is the algebraic sum of potentials due to +q and −q (superposition).
  • A dipole has charges +q, −q separated by 2a; net charge zero but nonzero dipole moment.
  • Dipole moment magnitude: p = 2qa; direction from −q to +q.
  • For a field point at distance r from centre making angle θ with p, potential depends on both r and θ.
  • In far field (r ≫ a), dipole potential falls as 1/r², faster than point charge (1/r).

🖼️Diagram

Point P at (r, θ) from dipole centre; distances to charges are r1, r2.

🎯Key Concepts

  • ➕Superposition (scalar): V = k(q/r1 − q/r2)
  • 📐Geometry: r1² = r² + a² − 2arcosθ; r2² = r² + a² + 2arcosθ
  • 🧾Far-field binomial: 1/r1 ≈ (1/r)(1 + (a/r)cosθ), 1/r2 ≈ (1/r)(1 − (a/r)cosθ)
  • 🎯Key result: V(r,θ) = kpcosθ/r² = k(p·r̂)/r² (for r ≫ a)
  • ⚖️Special planes: axis (θ=0,π) gives ±kp/r²; equatorial (θ=π/2) gives V=0

🔎DERIVATION (SUPERPOSITION → FAR FIELD)

  • Start: V = k(q/r1 − q/r2) (Eq. 2.9).
  • Use geometry of triangle OP with half-separation a: write r1², r2² (Eq. 2.10).
  • For r ≫ a, approximate: r1 ≈ r(1 − (a/r)cosθ), r2 ≈ r(1 + (a/r)cosθ).
  • Binomial to first order: 1/r1 ≈ (1/r)(1 + (a/r)cosθ), 1/r2 ≈ (1/r)(1 − (a/r)cosθ).
  • Subtract ⇒ V ≈ k(2qacosθ)/r² = kpcosθ/r² (Eq. 2.14).

⚙️SPECIAL DIRECTIONS + SIGN

  • Axis: θ = 0 ⇒ V = +kp/r² (point is on +q side).
  • Axis: θ = π ⇒ V = −kp/r² (point is on −q side).
  • Equatorial plane: θ = π/2 ⇒ cosθ = 0 ⇒ V = 0 (equal & opposite contributions).
  • Same |V| can occur on a cone about dipole axis (fixed θ, varying azimuth).
  • Validity: far-field formula needs r ≫ a; otherwise use exact Eq. 2.9.

POTENTIAL OF CHARGE SYSTEMS

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Electric potential at a point due to many charges is the scalar sum of potentials from each charge element (with sign).
  • For point charges, add V terms directly because potential has no direction (unlike E).
  • For a continuous distribution, replace summation by integration using dq = ρ(r) dv.
  • Standard reference: V(∞)=0, so contributions use distance from each charge element to the field point.
  • For a uniformly charged spherical shell: outside behaves like a point charge at centre; inside V is constant (not zero).

🖼️Diagram

At field point P, potentials add algebraically; for a spherical shell, V is constant inside and 1/r outside.

🎯Key Concepts

  • ➕Superposition for potential: V adds algebraically (Eq. 2.17, 2.18).
  • 🧮Sign comes from source charge: +q gives +V, −q gives −V.
  • 🧊Continuous charges: V(P)=k∫(dq/r) with dq=ρdv.
  • 🥚Spherical shell: V inside constant = surface value; E inside zero.
  • 📉For a point charge: V∝1/r falls slower than E∝1/r2.

🔎SUPERPOSITION (POINT CHARGES): Eq. 2.17 & Eq. 2.18

  • For charges q1…qn, distances riP: V(P)=V1+…+Vn.
  • Vi(P)=k qi/riP, so V(P)=k Σ(qi/riP).
  • Because V is scalar: do not resolve components; just keep charge signs.
  • If reference changes by constant C, all potentials shift by C, but differences stay same.

⚙️CONTINUOUS DISTRIBUTION & SPHERICAL SHELL RESULT

  • Split charge into elements: dq=ρ(r)dv (or σdA, λdl) and sum via integration.

  • Uniform spherical shell (radius R, total charge q): for r≥R, V(r)=kq/r.
  • For r≤R: V(r)=kq/R (constant everywhere inside since E=0).

POTENTIAL APPLICATIONS: ZERO-V POINTS & SIGN LOGIC (V, U, WORK)

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Electric potential at a point is the work done by an external agent per unit + test charge from ∞ (V(∞)=0).
  • For point charges, V is a scalar: V(P) = kΣ(qi/ri) with algebraic signs.
  • A zero-potential point satisfies Σ(qi/ri) = 0; it depends on the chosen reference.
  • Potential energy of a test charge: U = qV; sign flips if test charge is negative.
  • Work by electric field: Wfield = −ΔU = q(Vi − Vf); external work (slow move) is −Wfield.

🖼️Diagram

Two charges on x-axis: solve V=0 separately in regions I (left), II (between), III (right).

🎯Key Concepts

  • 📍For unlike charges, V = 0 always occurs between them; an additional zero may occur outside if magnitudes are unequal.
  • 🧮Write correct distances region-wise: between charges r=x and (d−x); right side r=x and (x−d); left side r=−x and (d−x).
  • ➕V adds algebraically; E adds vectorially (do not mix zero-V with zero-E).
  • 🔁Sign chain: decide sign of V, then U = qV, then Wfield = −ΔU.
  • 🧲Potential decreases along E: dV/dl = −E (component along the path).

🔎ZERO-POTENTIAL POINTS FOR TWO CHARGES (ON A LINE)

  • Let charges be q1 at x = 0 and q2 at x = d.
  • Potential on x-axis: V(x) = k(q1|x| + q2|x−d|).
  • Set V(x)=0 ⇒ q1r1 = −q2r2; requires q1 and q2 of opposite signs (for a finite solution).
  • Region II (0<x<d): r1=x, r2=d−x gives a zero between charges.
  • A second zero (outside) occurs only if |q1| ≠ |q2| and lies nearer the smaller-magnitude charge.

⚙️SIGN LOGIC: V, U = qV, AND WORK (FIELD vs EXTERNAL)

  • For a + source charge: V is positive and increases as you go closer (1/r).
  • For a − source charge: V is negative and becomes more negative as you go closer.
  • Potential energy: if test charge is negative, U decreases where V increases (since U=qV).
  • Work by field: Wfield = q(Vi − Vf); positive Wfield tends to increase kinetic energy.
  • Slow transport: Wext = ΔU = −Wfield (no change in kinetic energy).

CAPACITANCE

📖Capacitor & Capacitance

  • A capacitor consists of two conductors separated by an insulator.
  • Its plates carry equal and opposite charges Q and −Q; the capacitor's net charge is zero.
  • If the plate potentials are V1 and V2, then V = V1 − V2.
  • Capacitance is the charge stored per unit potential difference.
C = QV
C depends on geometry and the dielectric medium—not on Q or V separately.

ENERGY OF CHARGE CONFIGURATIONS

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Electrostatic potential energy U of a configuration = work done by an external agent in assembling charges from ∞ (quasi-static).
  • Choose reference U(∞)=0; only differences in U are physical (additive constant allowed).
  • For quasi-static assembly: Wext=ΔU and Wfield=−ΔU.
  • Because Coulomb force is conservative, the final U depends only on the final positions; any assembly order gives the same total U.
  • Sign meaning for two charges: like charges ⇒ U>0; unlike charges ⇒ U<0 (for finite separation, with U(∞)=0).

🖼️Diagram

🎯Key Concepts

  • 🧲Assembly idea: first charge costs 0 work; later charges cost q×(potential due to already placed charges).
  • ➕Two charges: U=kq1q2r12.
  • 🧩Many charges: sum over all distinct pairs once: U=kΣi<jqiqjrij.
  • 🔁Scalar nature: energy is algebraic sum; keep signs of qiqj.
  • 📉Distance effect (fixed charges): increasing rij reduces |U| (inverse proportionality).

🔎TWO-CHARGE ENERGY + SIGN

  • Place q1 first: W1=0 (no pre-existing field).
  • Bring q2 to separation r12: W2=q2V1=kq1q2r12.
  • So U=kq1q2r12; if q1q2>0 ⇒ repulsion ⇒ U>0.
  • If q1q2<0 ⇒ attraction helps assembly ⇒ Wext<0 ⇒ U<0 (with U(∞)=0).

⚙️MULTI-CHARGE ENERGY + SQUARE GEOMETRY TIP

  • For 3 charges: U=k(q1q2r12+q1q3r13+q2q3r23).
  • General rule: sum over all distinct pairs once; do NOT double count (i<j).
  • Square of side d: edge distance = d, diagonal distance = √2d.
  • Alternate signs on square corners: edge pairs give negative terms, diagonal pairs give positive terms.

ENERGY IN EXTERNAL FIELDS

📖Definition & Introduction

  • For a charge q placed in a given external potential V(r), its potential energy is U(r) = qV(r) (with chosen reference).
  • This external-field energy is different from system energy stored due to mutual interactions among charges.
  • Energy change on moving q from A to B is ΔU = qΔV = q(VB−VA).
  • In quasi-static motion, work by external agent Wext = ΔU, and work by field Wfield = −ΔU.
  • Electron volt: 1 eV is the energy gained by charge magnitude e moving through 1 V; 1 eV = 1.602×10−19 J.
  • For two charges in an external potential, total energy includes mutual term plus each qV term at its location.

🖼️Diagram

🎯Key Concepts

  • 🎯Given V(r) means produced by sources not counted in the system’s mutual energy.
  • 🧩System energy (two free charges): U12=14πϵ0q1q2r12.
  • 📍External energy of a charge at point: U=qV (Eq. 2.27 idea).
  • ↔️Energy gained/lost depends on sign: for q>0, moving to higher V increases U; for electron it decreases.
  • 🔁Only ΔU matters; zero level set by choosing reference (often V(∞)=0).

🔎SYSTEM ENERGY vs ENERGY IN A GIVEN EXTERNAL POTENTIAL

  • External-field energy: sources creating V are outside the system; use U=qV.
  • Mutual (system) energy: charges are part of system; for two charges, U12=14πϵ0q1q2r12.
  • If external sources are included, total energy changes; avoid double counting by defining the system clearly.
  • In external field, changing reference adds constant to U via V; physics depends on ΔU only.

⚙️TWO CHARGES IN AN EXTERNAL FIELD (Eq. 2.29 IDEA)

  • Let external potential be V(r); place q1 at r1, q2 at r2.
  • Total potential energy: U = 14πϵ0q1q2r12 + q1V(r1) + q2V(r2).
  • Work to separate them to infinity in same external field: ΔU = U(∞)−U(initial); include changes in V at their final positions.
  • If external V is uniform and charges end at same potentials as start, only mutual term changes.

TOPIC12
Electrostatic equilibrium in conductors: E=0 inside, charge on surface, V constant, field normal

CONDUCTORS IN ELECTROSTATICS

📖Definition & Introduction

  • Electrostatic equilibrium of a conductor: free charges are at rest, so macroscopic current is zero.
  • In equilibrium, the electric field inside a conductor is zero; otherwise charges would keep drifting.
  • Any excess charge resides only on the outer surface of an isolated conductor.
  • The conductor (including its surface) is an equipotential: V is same at every interior point.
  • Just outside the conductor, E is perpendicular to the surface; tangential component must be zero.

🖼️Diagram

In electrostatic equilibrium: inside conductor E=0 and V is uniform; outside field is normal to surface.

🎯Key Concepts

  • 🧲Conductors have free electrons; in an applied E, electrons drift opposite to E.
  • ⏸️Equilibrium condition: no net force on free charges ⇒ Einside=0.
  • 🧱If Etangent at surface ≠ 0, surface charges would move ⇒ impossible in equilibrium.
  • 🟣V constant inside conductor: moving a test charge inside requires zero work.
  • 🧾Gauss-law idea: for a Gaussian surface fully inside conductor, flux is zero ⇒ enclosed charge is zero.

🔎WHY E=0 INSIDE (PHYSICAL ARGUMENT)

  • If E inside ≠ 0, each free electron feels force F=−eE and accelerates.
  • Charges rearrange until their own field cancels the internal field ⇒ net E becomes zero.
  • Equilibrium means no further redistribution; hence no current and no internal field.

⚙️GAUSS-LAW CONSEQUENCES (EXCESS CHARGE ON SURFACE)

  • Take a Gaussian surface wholly inside conductor: since E=0 everywhere on it, flux = 0.
  • By Gauss law, enclosed charge must be zero ⇒ no net excess charge in the bulk.
  • Any added (excess) charge must reside on the outer surface of an isolated conductor.

  • Electrostatic equilibrium implies no motion of free charges; otherwise equilibrium is broken.
  • Einside=0 for a conductor in equilibrium, even if it carries net charge.
  • Excess charge lies on the outer surface of an isolated conductor (bulk enclosed charge is zero).
  • At the surface, E is perpendicular; tangential component is zero.
  • V is same everywhere inside and on the conductor surface (equipotential body).
